\section*{Abstract}
Manual scoring of mouse sleep from electroencephalography (EEG) and electromyography (EMG) lacks consensus across laboratories, and supervised deep learning models trained at one site often fail on data from others until retrained on multi-laboratory labels.
We present an unsupervised framework for population-level sleep staging on the Mouse Sleep Staging Validation dataset (MSSV; OpenNeuro ds006366).
On identical spectral inputs we compare decoder-only conditional cGMVAE and cHMM--GMVAE (primary pair), with HMMGMVAE and time-domain HMM(raw) as additional ladder rungs where validation is complete.
Models train on leave-mice-out folds pooling laboratories 2, 3, and 5; held-out mice are staged by zero-shot inference---encoding without subject identifiers and assigning states from the learned prior only, without per-mouse manual calibration.
On joint cross-lab holdout, cHMM--GMVAE reaches mean best-of-three prior normalised mutual information (NMI) 0.58 (range 0.40--0.65) versus 0.28 for matched cGMVAE ($\Delta = +0.30$ on every fold).
Within-laboratory holdout shows site-specific gains: temporal priors help most on laboratories 3 and 5, while static mixture decoders remain competitive on laboratory 2.
A substage resolution sweep selects $K{=}4$ states whose spectra, $\theta/\delta$ ratios, bout lengths, and transition structure align with Wake, NREM, a transition-enriched state, and REM.
Decoder-only conditioning with temporal mixture priors thus improves unsupervised generalisation across mice and acquisition sites while supporting biologically interpretable substage discovery.

\section*{Author summary}
We study how \textit{Mus musculus} sleep can be classified automatically from brain and muscle signals when recordings come from different laboratories with different equipment.
Expert human scorers disagree across labs, and supervised computer models often fail on new sites.
We built a family of unsupervised models that learn shared sleep patterns from many mice, then test them on held-out animals they have never seen---without asking a human to calibrate each new recording.
Our best model combines a neural network that reads EEG and EMG with a temporal prior that enforces realistic sleep bout structure.
It outperforms simpler models on cross-lab holdout and discovers finer sleep substates whose brain-wave profiles match known physiology.
We validate substages with power spectra, muscle activity, and bout lengths, with guidance from sleep neuroscientists.
This work offers a label-free route to comparing sleep across studies and may help researchers find transition states between wake, NREM, and REM that are hard to score by eye alone.

\section*{Introduction}
Rodent sleep is scored from EEG and EMG into wake, non-rapid eye movement (NREM), and rapid eye movement (REM), yet montage and laboratory protocols differ across studies~\cite{rose2025mssv}.
Rose et al.\ show that manual scoring lacks cross-lab consensus and that published deep models generalise poorly across MSSV sites until retrained on multi-laboratory data~\cite{rose2026consensus}.
This motivates label-agnostic staging evaluated under strict population holdout rather than per-animal calibration.
Three methodological families address complementary gaps.
\textbf{Supervised ceilings} such as SPINDLE and REST achieve high cross-lab accuracy but require expert labels that inherit rater noise~\cite{miladinovic2019spindle,wang2026rest}.
\textbf{Transductive unsupervised} tools including AccuSleep and SegWay fit or calibrate each new recording individually---for example, AccuSleep requires roughly ten minutes of manually scored baseline per mouse~\cite{barger2019accusleep,yaghouby2016segway}.
\textbf{Static unsupervised} methods including FASTER, FASTER2, mcRBM, and WUCSS discover clusters or substates without deep latent sequence priors on multi-laboratory holdout protocols~\cite{sunagawa2013faster,yamada2024faster2,katsageorgiou2018mcRBM,cusinato2024wucss}.
None combine decoder-only population training, sticky HMM--GMM temporal priors, and zero-shot cross-laboratory evaluation on MSSV.

Our MSc thesis established that subject-conditioned GMVAEs reach population NMI $\approx 0.70$ on a curated ten-mouse, single-laboratory leave-one-subject-out protocol with encoder+decoder conditioning~\cite{elmgreen2026thesis}.
\textbf{This paper} extends that line with decoder-only conditioning on a curated 20-mouse cohort from laboratories 2, 3, and 5 (6+10+4 mice; quality-screened from 92 MSSV recordings), zero-shot holdout inference without subject identifiers at staging, and end-to-end warm HMM--GMM priors~\cite{dilokthanakul2016gmvae,sohn2015cvae,johnson2016svae,ebbers2017hmmvae}.
We contribute: (i)~decoder-only cVAE training with zero-shot holdout staging; (ii)~a locked fair comparison of cGMVAE versus cHMM--GMVAE on identical manifests; and (iii)~substage biology after a resolution sweep with physiology and transition readouts~\cite{katsageorgiou2018mcRBM,somnotate2024}.
Dataset, protocol, and model summaries are in \nameref{sec:data} and \nameref{sec:methods}; the broader thesis-to-report research arc is in \nameref{S7_Appendix_arc}; full prior derivations and the thesis $K{=}13$ taxonomy are in \nameref{S1_Appendix} and \nameref{S6_Fig}.

\section*{Data}
\label{sec:data}

\methodpara{Dataset.}
All experiments used the Mouse Sleep Staging Validation dataset (MSSV, OpenNeuro ds006366)~\cite{rose2025mssv}.
Recordings comprise invasive EEG and neck EMG from 92 mice acquired across five European laboratories under a shared protocol, with expert annotations on a fixed 4\,s grid for Wake, NREM, REM, and Artifact.
We analyse laboratories 2 (Bern), 3 (Copenhagen), and 5 (Lyon) only: they span three acquisition sites with comparable healthy wild-type cohorts and complementary montages, while laboratories 1 and 4 were excluded to avoid mixing pathology-specific or single-electrode cohorts with the cross-lab holdout design~\cite{elmgreen2026thesis}.
Expert labels are used for evaluation only and are not supplied to the unsupervised objectives.
All data were previously published. No new animal procedures were performed.

\methodpara{Quality screening and cohort size.}
Following thesis quality criteria extended to all three labs~\cite{elmgreen2026thesis}, we retained mice whose EEG channels pass amplitude and spectral checks and whose EMG separates wake from sleep.
Per channel, relative low-frequency power (0.5--2\,Hz over 0.5--30\,Hz) must be $\leq 0.2$ on every retained EEG electrode (EEG1 and EEG2 at labs~3/5; EEG1 and EEG4 at lab~2).
EMG awake-over-NREM root-mean-square ratio must be $\geq 2$ on every retained channel.
These thresholds reject drift-dominated EEG and weak atonia cues before cross-lab training.
After screening, \textbf{20 mice} remain (6 in lab~2, 10 in lab~3, 4 in lab~5) in manifest \texttt{cv\_quality\_cohort\_v1.yaml}.

\methodpara{Montages and channel locks.}
Montages differ by site but were harmonised for modelling.
\textbf{Lab~2} (Bern) uses four implanted electrodes (two parietal, two frontal) plus neck EMG; we model \textbf{EEG1 (parietal) + EEG4 (frontal) + EMG} to match the parietal--frontal differential used at labs 3 and 5.
\textbf{Labs~3 and~5} each use \textbf{EEG1 (parietal) + EEG2 (frontal) + EMG}.
Per-laboratory spectral band limits and preprocessing locks (EEG lowpass, EMG bandpass, optional post-normalisation) were fixed from incohort ablation winners (\nameref{S4_Appendix}).

\methodpara{Epoch construction and preprocessing.}
Continuous recordings were segmented into 4\,s epochs at 128\,Hz sampling rate; Artifact epochs were removed before modelling.
Montage and acquisition site are treated as nuisance factors controlled by these fixed locks rather than per-mouse manual calibration.

\methodpara{Input representation.}
Each epoch is a log-power spectral map with channels $c \in \{1,\ldots,C\}$ and frequency bins $f$, giving tensors $\mathbf{x}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times F}$ batched into contiguous sequences for temporal models.
The deep ladder learns staging directly from these spectral inputs rather than hand-crafted expert features.

\section*{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection*{Model ladder and architecture}

\methodpara{Model ladder.}
The primary comparison pairs decoder-only cGMVAE (static GMM prior, $T{=}1$) with decoder-only cHMM--GMVAE (warm sticky HMM--GMM prior, $T{=}64$) on identical manifests, checkpoints, and $K{=}3$ mixture components for macro staging.
Both conditional models use subject embeddings in the decoder only during training; HMMGMVAE without conditioning serves as a sequence-prior baseline in \nameref{S1_Fig}~\cite{ebbers2017hmmvae}.
Locked recipes fix latent dimension $d{=}6$, embedding dimension 8, learning rate $1.3 \times 10^{-3}$, 200 epochs, batch size 128, and three random seeds; full hyperparameters are in \nameref{S1_File}.

\methodpara{Encoder--decoder.}
Training batches consist of contiguous windows $\mathbf{x}_{1:T} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times C \times F}$ with $T=64$ epochs per sequence.
A convolutional encoder maps each epoch independently to a diagonal Gaussian posterior,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:encoder}
q_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\mathbf{z}_t \mid \mathbf{x}_t) = \mathcal{N}\!\left(\mathbf{z}_t \mid \boldsymbol{\mu}_t, \operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_t^2)\right),
\qquad
(\boldsymbol{\mu}_t, \log \boldsymbol{\sigma}_t^2) = f_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\mathbf{x}_t),
\end{equation}
where $f_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}$ is the CNN--MLP encoder and $\mathbf{z}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ with $d=6$.
Latent samples use the reparameterisation $\mathbf{z}_t = \boldsymbol{\mu}_t + \boldsymbol{\sigma}_t \odot \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t$, $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$.
The decoder reconstructs each epoch from $\mathbf{z}_t$ and, during training only, a subject embedding $\mathbf{e}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{8}$ for mouse $i$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:decoder}
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t = g_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\!\left(\mathbf{z}_t, \mathbf{e}_i\right).
\end{equation}
Under decoder-only conditioning, $f_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}$ does not receive $\mathbf{e}_i$; only $g_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$ does~\cite{elmgreen2026thesis}.
The CNN uses kernel sizes $[7,5,5,3]$ on the encoder and the reverse on the decoder; MLP hidden widths are $[512,256,128]$ (encoder) and $[128,256,512]$ (decoder).

\methodpara{Variational objective and prior.}
Training minimises reconstruction error plus a $\beta$-weighted KL term ($\beta$ ramps from 0.01 to 1.0).
cGMVAE uses a static Gaussian mixture prior; cHMM--GMVAE replaces it with a sticky HMM--GMM prior ($\kappa{=}0.92$) warm-started from encoder means (\nameref{S1_Appendix})~\cite{ebbers2017hmmvae,johnson2016svae}.

\methodpara{Prior-based staging at holdout.}
At validation, emissions are evaluated on encoder means $\boldsymbol{\mu}_t$ rather than sampled $\mathbf{z}_t$.
Static cGMVAE assigns $\hat{s}_t = \arg\max_k \left[\log \pi_k + \log b_k(\boldsymbol{\mu}_t)\right]$.
cHMM--GMVAE applies Viterbi decoding on $B_{tk} = \log b_k(\boldsymbol{\mu}_t)$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:viterbi}
\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{1:T} = \arg\max_{s_{1:T}} \left[\log \pi_{s_1} + \sum_{t=2}^{T}\log A_{s_{t-1}, s_t} + \sum_{t=1}^{T}\log b_{s_t}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_t)\right],
\end{equation}
contrasting with transductive per-animal calibration~\cite{barger2019accusleep,yaghouby2016segway}.

\subsection*{Cross-validation and holdout protocol}

\methodpara{Evaluation scopes.}
The 20 quality-screened mice were partitioned into four folds with leave-mice-out cross-validation.
Three folds define the training pool and one fold defines held-out validation mice that do not appear in training.
Joint scope pools all training-fold mice from laboratories 2, 3, and 5 into one population model and tests on the held-out fold across sites.
Within-lab scope restricts both training and validation to a single laboratory while using the same fold splits.
Absolute prior NMI values between joint and within-lab scopes are not compared because the training distributions differ.

\methodpara{Holdout inference.}
Held-out mice are staged without subject identifiers and without decoder reconstruction.
The encoder produces deterministic means $\boldsymbol{\mu}_t$ from spectral inputs only; subject embeddings and the decoder are not used.
State labels are assigned from the trained GMM or sticky HMM--GMM prior on $\boldsymbol{\mu}_t$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:viterbi}; prior details in \nameref{S1_Appendix}).
Per-laboratory spectral locks remain fixed at holdout.

\methodpara{Agreement metrics.}
Agreement with expert Wake, NREM, and REM labels was quantified primarily using normalised mutual information (NMI)~\cite{elmgreen2026thesis}.
For partitions $Y$ and $\hat{Y}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:nmi}
\mathrm{NMI}(Y,\hat{Y}) = \frac{2\,I(Y;\hat{Y})}{H(Y)+H(\hat{Y})},
\end{equation}
where $I$ denotes mutual information and $H$ Shannon entropy.
NMI is sensitive to cluster structure even when class frequencies differ and is our main holdout metric.

\methodpara{Macro-aligned accuracy (appendix).}
Macro accuracy maps each predicted cluster to its \emph{dominant} expert macro label (Hungarian alignment when $K{=}3$), then reports the fraction of epochs matching expert Wake/NREM/REM.
This can exceed chance and diverge from NMI when (i)~$K>3$ substates collapse onto the same macro label, (ii)~a model predicts too few active clusters (e.g.\ fold~4 cGMVAE), or (iii)~clusters are macro-pure but permuted.
We therefore treat accuracy as a complementary, interpretable ceiling---not equivalent to NMI---and report both in \nameref{S4_Table} and \nameref{S5_Table}.

Reported holdout NMI uses the maximum prior NMI across three random seeds for each fold and model configuration (mean over folds in tables), because random initialisation induces seed-level instability in unsupervised training.

\subsection*{Training, substages, and reproducibility}

\methodpara{Decoder-only conditioning.}
Subject identity enters only the decoder path (Eq.~\eqref{eq:decoder}), not the encoder (Eq.~\eqref{eq:encoder}), unlike the thesis encoder+decoder setup~\cite{elmgreen2026thesis}.
Nuisance amplitude and montage effects are absorbed during reconstruction while encoder means remain free of identity information for holdout staging.

\methodpara{Training and checkpoints.}
Models trained on all mice in the training folds; validation prior NMI monitored each epoch and the best checkpoint per seed retained.
Macro ladder comparisons use $K{=}3$ components; a substage sweep varies $K \in \{3,4,\ldots,15\}$ on joint holdout fold~4.
We selected $K{=}4$ as the highest $K$ within one standard deviation of the peak mean NMI with non-degenerate occupancy across seeds (Fig~\ref{fig2}).
Physiology summaries use EEG power spectra, $\theta/\delta$ ratio, bout length, expert macro composition, and row-normalised transition matrices~\cite{katsageorgiou2018mcRBM}.

\methodpara{Data and code availability.}
MSSV is available on OpenNeuro (ds006366).
Processed features, fold manifests, and locked configuration files are in the SPA repository (\nameref{S1_File}).
Training, evaluation, and figure generation code are maintained in the SPA repository; a version-tagged Zenodo archive will be released before journal submission.

\subsection*{Substage resolution and physiology}

Macro ladder comparisons use $K{=}3$.
On joint holdout fold~4 we swept $K \in \{3,4,\ldots,15\}$ (Fig~\ref{fig2}): mean validation NMI peaks at $K{=}4$ (best seed 0.643; $K{=}3$ at 0.635), while mean log prior density $\log p(\mathbf{z}_{1:T})/(T\cdot L)$ is highest near $K{=}4$--$5$ before NMI declines beyond $K{=}6$, indicating over-segmentation relative to expert macro labels.
Wake and NREM states separate by $\theta/\delta$ and bout length; a transition-enriched state shows mixed expert macro composition at Wake--NREM boundaries; the fourth state aligns with REM spectra (\nameref{S5_Fig}).

\subsection*{Limitations}

Agreement is quantified by NMI against expert macro labels, which inherit cross-laboratory disagreement~\cite{rose2026consensus}; high NMI therefore measures discovery of shared structure, not mimicry of a single rater.
Appendix macro accuracy can read higher than NMI implies because dominant-label mapping rewards macro-pure clusters even when cluster boundaries differ from expert scoring.
Absolute holdout NMI (0.58 mean) remains below thesis leave-one-subject-out performance on a curated single-lab subset because cross-laboratory shift is harder than within-lab LOSO~\cite{elmgreen2026thesis}.
Seed and fold variance (fold~2) indicate remaining fragility.
Fixed spectral front-end locks control laboratory nuisance but do not replace per-mouse calibration; the trade-off is intentional zero-shot staging without manual baseline scoring.
Decoder-only conditioning is the main advance over thesis encoder+decoder conditioning for population holdout; ablation evidence is in \nameref{S3_Appendix}.

\section*{Conclusion}

Decoder-only conditional HMM--GMVAE with sticky mixture priors improves zero-shot cross-laboratory mouse sleep staging on MSSV relative to matched cGMVAE (mean prior NMI 0.58 vs.\ 0.28).
Subject embeddings at training explain nuisance variability without leaking identity at holdout inference; temporal priors capture bout structure where laboratory montages support sequence modelling.
Substage physiology and transition analyses at $K{=}4$ extend macro staging toward interpretable Wake, NREM, transition, and REM structure under rigorous population holdout.

\subsection*{Full HMM--GMM prior and warm-start}
\label{S1_Appendix}

Each discrete latent state $s_t \in \{1,\ldots,K\}$ emits a diagonal Gaussian in $\mathbb{R}^{d}$.
The per-step emission log-density is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:emission}
\log b_k(\mathbf{z}_t) = \log \mathcal{N}\!\left(\mathbf{z}_t \mid \mathbf{m}_k, \operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{v}_k)\right).
\end{equation}
Transitions are initialised sticky with $\kappa = 0.92$.
The marginal log-likelihood of a latent window is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:forward}
\log p(\mathbf{z}_{1:T}) = \log \sum_{s_{1:T}} \pi_{s_1} \prod_{t=2}^{T} A_{s_{t-1}, s_t} \prod_{t=1}^{T} b_{s_t}(\mathbf{z}_t),
\end{equation}
and the sequence KL averages
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kl_hmm}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KL}} = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left[\log q_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\mathbf{z}_t \mid \mathbf{x}_t) - \log p(\mathbf{z}_{1:T})\right].
\end{equation}
Warm-start schedule: epochs 1--17 use standard Gaussian KL; epoch 18 initialises GMM emissions from $K$-means on encoder means; epochs 18--36 use independent mixture KL; from epoch 37 the HMM--GMM prior blends in over 18 ramp epochs~\cite{ebbers2017hmmvae,johnson2016svae}.
CNN architecture: channel widths $[32,64,128,128]$, kernel sizes $[7,5,5,3]$, strides 2, MLP widths $[512,256,128]$.
