\justifying
\noindent
Cross-laboratory manual scoring of mouse sleep from electroencephalography (EEG) and electromyography (EMG) lacks consensus, and supervised models trained at one site often fail elsewhere without retraining or per-animal calibration.
To stage held-out animals without that calibration, we study twenty quality-screened wild-type mice from three European Mouse Sleep Staging Validation (MSSV) laboratories under leave-mice-out cross-validation with four folds.
Using spectral EEG and EMG alone as input, decoder-only conditional variational autoencoders (cVAE) assign states through the encoder and a learned mixture prior at inference.
When the prior adds bout structure via a hidden Markov model--Gaussian mixture model (HMM--GMM), mean prior normalized mutual information (NMI) reaches 0.575 on joint cross-laboratory holdout versus 0.282 for a matched static mixture, a gain of 0.29 NMI averaged over folds.
To explore finer substages, we swept mixture count on fold~4 holdout mice and on the full population. Prior log-density peaks at $K{=}8$ on the cohort of twenty mice, and we qualitatively analyse the resulting substates from physiology and transition structure. 
We propose an interpretable organisation with three Wake substates, two NREM substates, one REM substate, and one short-lived transition substate (Figs~\ref{fig3}--\ref{fig5}). 
Without expert labels at staging, the approach supports cross-laboratory comparison of proposed substage structure beyond Wake, NREM, and REM, before scoring protocols are harmonised.
\end{minipage}
\end{center}

\section*{Author summary}
Sleep biologists score mouse EEG and EMG into Wake, NREM, and rapid eye movement (REM) sleep, but raters at different laboratories often disagree.
We trained unsupervised models on mice from Bern, Copenhagen, and Lyon, then tested them on held-out animals the models had never seen, without calibrating each new recording by hand.
Our best model reads spectral brain and muscle signals through a neural network and enforces realistic sleep bout structure with a temporal prior.
Our aim was to stage sleep without expert labels and to propose substages from the data.
It outperformed simpler baselines on cross-laboratory holdout and recovered an eight-substage population map whose spectra and bout lengths matched patterns we discussed with sleep neuroscientists at the Kornum laboratory, indicating structure beyond Wake, NREM, and REM alone.

\papersection{Introduction}

Manual mouse sleep scores disagree across MSSV laboratories, and supervised models trained at one site fail on held-out animals elsewhere unless retrained or calibrated per mouse~\cite{rose2026consensus,miladinovic2019spindle,wang2026rest,barger2019accusleep,yaghouby2016segway}.
Expert staging nonetheless maps continuous physiology into Wake, NREM, and REM, the standard three-class benchmark.
Unsupervised work in mice has resolved substages with distinct spectra, bout lengths, and transition roles that Wake/NREM/REM labels alone do not name~\cite{katsageorgiou2018mcRBM}.
Because montage and laboratory protocols differ across studies~\cite{rose2025mssv}, claims that sleep structure extends beyond macro scoring must be tested on new animals and new sites under population holdout without per-animal calibration at inference.

Unsupervised mouse EEG/EMG workflows can discover latent states from hand-crafted or learned features, often with HMM-style temporal structure~\cite{cusinato2024wucss}, but rarely under leave-mice-out holdout across laboratories in a shared population model.
Katsageorgiou et al.\ resolved rodent substages beyond the macro taxonomy with distinct physiology and bout structure in mcRBM~\cite{katsageorgiou2018mcRBM}, yet without cross-laboratory leave-mice-out holdout.
By contrast, Garc\'ia Ciudad et al.\ showed that longer temporal context improves supervised rodent staging, especially for REM~\cite{garciaciudad2024temporal}, but that setting remains fully supervised and does not test unsupervised cross-site transfer.
In our MSc thesis~\cite{elmgreen2026thesis}, we developed mixture-VAE staging on Copenhagen mice alone with encoder+decoder conditioning and expert-engineered features.
That work reached similarly high prior NMI under the same $K{=}3$ macro grid of about 0.7 NMI.
This report uses decoder-only conditioning and a spectral CNN front-end under cross-laboratory leave-mice-out holdout, a strictly harder evaluation.
Expert labels are used for post-hoc evaluation only.

We extend decoder-only cVAE with mixture priors: a static GMM for cGMVAE~\cite{dilokthanakul2016gmvae}, decoder conditioning~\cite{sohn2015cvae}, and a sticky HMM--GMM sequence prior composed with the encoder~\cite{johnson2016svae}.
This type of model has not been applied on our sleep data before. Subject embeddings condition reconstruction during training only, not the encoder mapping from spectra to latents.
The encoder therefore represents sleep-state structure shared across animals, while the decoder learns site-specific amplitude and montage signatures needed for reconstruction.
Held-out mice are staged from encoder means and the learned prior without subject identifiers.
We first validate zero-shot cross-laboratory holdout at $K{=}3$, then ask whether latents support finer structure than Wake, NREM, and REM through mixture-resolution sweeps and physiology-first interpretation~\cite{katsageorgiou2018mcRBM,elmgreen2026thesis}.
On locked spectral inputs from laboratories 2, 3, and 5 at Bern, Copenhagen, and Lyon, we compare a static Gaussian mixture model (GMM) prior with a warm conditional HMM--GMM variational autoencoder (cHMM--GMVAE) prior.
Holdout fold~4 is the primary generalisation benchmark for macro metrics, whereas the full cohort supports substage physiology interpretation in Results (Figs~\ref{fig4} and~\ref{fig5}) and supplementary PCA panels.
An overview roadmap linking analyses, figures, and appendix panels is in \apdx{app:fig:story_map}.
Dataset and protocol details are in Section~\ref{sec:data} and Appendix~A (Tables~\ref{app:tab:cohort} and~\ref{app:tab:splits}).

\papersection{Data}
\label{sec:data}

\methodpara{Dataset and cohort.}
MSSV on OpenNeuro ds006366~\cite{rose2025mssv} comprises invasive EEG and neck EMG from 92 mice across five European laboratories.
Expert Wake, NREM, REM, and Artifact labels are provided on a 4\,s grid for evaluation only.
We analyse laboratories 2 at Bern, 3 at Copenhagen, and 5 at Lyon.
These are healthy wild-type sites with parietal--frontal invasive EEG and neck EMG comparable across sites.
We exclude laboratory~1 (Bologna), which uses a single ipsilateral fronto-parietal differential rather than separate parietal and frontal contacts, and laboratory~4 (Copenhagen pathology cohort), which uses prefrontal--cortical montages not matched to the Bern/Lyon layout~\cite{rose2025mssv}.
Quality screening removes mice with unstable low-frequency EEG and weak EMG Wake/NREM contrast before modelling.
Drift is quantified as relative 0.5--2\,Hz power $\leq 0.2$ of 0.5--30\,Hz on each retained electrode.
EMG must separate Wake from NREM with a Wake/NREM root-mean-square ratio $\geq 2$.
The screened cohort comprises 20 mice with 6, 10, and 4 per site and ${\sim}453$\,h of recording (per-mouse inventory in \apdx{app:tab:cohort}).
We use four-fold leave-mice-out cross-validation at the mouse level (fold assignments in \apdx{app:tab:splits}).
Lyon contributes only four quality-screened mice, which caps the fold count while keeping every fold a strict subject holdout.
Training mice never appear in the validation fold for a given split.
No new animal procedures were performed.

\methodpara{Preprocessing and inputs.}
Montages are harmonised to a parietal--frontal EEG differential plus neck EMG.
Bern (laboratory~2) records four invasive EEG channels, two parietal and two frontal, following MSSV cohort~B~\cite{rose2025mssv}.
Copenhagen and Lyon each use one parietal and one frontal contact (EEG1 and EEG2).
At Bern we use EEG1 (parietal) and EEG4 (frontal) so the pairing matches the parietal--frontal layout at laboratories~3 and~5.
That pair was fixed from incohort ablation over the alternative frontal contact EEG3.
Locked passbands follow incohort winners: Bern EEG 0--30\,Hz and EMG 3--100\,Hz without post-normalisation, Copenhagen and Lyon EEG 0--20\,Hz and EMG 5--60\,Hz with run-wise post-normalisation (\apdx{app:incohort_ceiling}, Table~\ref{app:tab:incohort}, \apdx{app:data:bands}).
Each recipe was fixed from incohort ablations on training mice in that laboratory before any holdout run, not tuned on validation folds.
Recordings follow the MSSV 4\,s annotation grid at 128\,Hz sampling.
For cross-validation holdout runs, expert-labeled Artifact epochs are removed before training.
Each epoch is converted to a log-power spectral map $\mathbf{x}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times F}$ rather than raw voltages, because time-domain HMM baselines stay near chance on this cross-lab protocol (Appendix~B).
Batched epochs form contiguous sequences for temporal models.

\papersection{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\papersubsection{Model and training}

Spectral EEG/EMG epochs are mapped to a low-dimensional latent space by a cVAE.
Subject embeddings condition reconstruction during training only, not encoder inference.
Staging at holdout uses encoder means and a learned mixture prior without subject identifiers, so latents must encode cross-animal sleep structure rather than site-specific nuisance absorbed only in the decoder.
We compare three deep baselines on matched inputs and cross-validation folds.
cGMVAE uses a static GMM with $T{=}1$.
cHMM--GMVAE uses a warm HMM--GMM sequence prior with $T{=}64$ epochs, about 4.3\,min, and $K{=}3$ for staging.
HMMGMVAE uses the same CNN--VAE and warm HMM--GMM prior as cHMM--GMVAE but disables subject embeddings, isolating decoder-only conditioning while holding the sequence prior fixed.
All three share the same locked per-laboratory spectral preprocessing and training recipe (\apdx{app:methods:hyper}), with three seeds per fold.
Table~\ref{table1} contrasts cGMVAE with cHMM--GMVAE for the temporal prior and HMMGMVAE with cHMM--GMVAE for decoder-only subject conditioning at matched $T{=}64$.
cGMVAE uses $T{=}1$ because the static mixture scores epochs independently, whereas HMMGMVAE and cHMM--GMVAE use $T{=}64$ windows for the sequence prior.

\methodpara{Design choices.}
Inputs are log-power spectra with a CNN front-end because raw time-domain HMMs fail to generalise across mice on MSSV.
Latent dimension $d{=}6$ matches the thesis-scale mixture models, sequence length $T{=}64$ gives about 4.3\,min of bout context, and sticky self-transition weight $\kappa{=}0.92$ encourages persistent sleep states.
Fold~4 sensitivity checks showed shorter windows without the warm schedule collapsed holdout NMI (Appendix~B).
cHMM--GMVAE warm-starts from a Gaussian encoder, then a static mixture, then ramps in the HMM--GMM prior to avoid cold-start collapse.
Training minimises mean squared reconstruction on log-power maps with a ramped $\beta$ KL weight.
Checkpoints are selected without expert labels from validation log-likelihood and state-occupancy entropy (Appendix~B), and prior NMI is computed only after that selection.

\methodpara{Encoder-decoder.}
Training batches are contiguous windows $\mathbf{x}_{1:T} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times C \times F}$ with $T=64$.
A convolutional encoder maps each epoch to a diagonal Gaussian posterior,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:encoder}
q_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\mathbf{z}_t \mid \mathbf{x}_t) = \mathcal{N}\!\left(\mathbf{z}_t \mid \boldsymbol{\mu}_t, \operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_t^2)\right),
\qquad
(\boldsymbol{\mu}_t, \log \boldsymbol{\sigma}_t^2) = f_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\mathbf{x}_t),
\end{equation}
with $f_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}$ a CNN--multilayer perceptron (MLP) encoder and $\mathbf{z}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$, $d{=}6$.
Latent samples use the reparameterisation $\mathbf{z}_t = \boldsymbol{\mu}_t + \boldsymbol{\sigma}_t \odot \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t$ with $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$.
The decoder reconstructs each epoch from $\mathbf{z}_t$ and, during training only, a subject embedding $\mathbf{e}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{8}$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:decoder}
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t = g_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\!\left(\mathbf{z}_t, \mathbf{e}_i\right).
\end{equation}
Under decoder-only conditioning, $f_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}$ does not receive $\mathbf{e}_i$.
CNN and MLP widths are in \apdx{app:methods:hyper}.
Fig~\ref{fig_arch} summarizes the training and holdout staging path.

\methodpara{Training objective.}
For each window $\mathbf{x}_{1:T}$, training minimises
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:loss}
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{recon}} + \beta\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KL}},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:recon}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{recon}} = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\|\mathbf{x}_t - \hat{\mathbf{x}}_t\right\|_2^2
\end{equation}
is mean squared reconstruction error over $T$ contiguous 4\,s epochs.
The Kullback--Leibler (KL) term averages the log-density ratio between the encoder and the mixture prior over the window,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kl_hmm}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KL}} = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\log q_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\mathbf{z}_t \mid \mathbf{x}_t) - \log p(\mathbf{z}_{1:T}),
\end{equation}
where $\log p(\mathbf{z}_{1:T})$ is the joint prior marginal over the full sequence (not repeated per epoch).
For cHMM--GMVAE, $\log p(\mathbf{z}_{1:T})$ is the HMM--GMM forward marginal with emission densities $b_k(\mathbf{z}_t)$ and transition matrix $A$.
For cGMVAE, each epoch is scored independently with a static GMM marginal at $T{=}1$.
The weight $\beta$ is zero for the first ten epochs, then ramps linearly from 0.01 to 1.0.
cHMM--GMVAE warm-starts the mixture prior from encoder means (Appendix~B.4).
Phase timings and prior equations are in \apdx{app:ablations:hmm} and \apdx{app:methods:warmstart}.

\methodpara{Prior-based staging at holdout.}
Held-out mice are staged from encoder means $\boldsymbol{\mu}_t$ and the trained prior without subject embeddings or decoder reconstruction.
Using means rather than sampled latents reduces Viterbi variance across seeds once the encoder is trained.
Static cGMVAE assigns $\hat{s}_t = \arg\max_k \left[\log \pi_k + \log b_k(\boldsymbol{\mu}_t)\right]$.
cHMM--GMVAE applies Viterbi decoding,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:viterbi}
\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{1:T} = \arg\max_{s_{1:T}} \left[\log \pi_{s_1} + \sum_{t=2}^{T}\log A_{s_{t-1}, s_t} + \sum_{t=1}^{T}\log b_{s_t}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_t)\right],
\end{equation}
with $B_{tk} = \log b_k(\boldsymbol{\mu}_t)$.

\papersubsection{Evaluation protocol}

The twenty quality-screened mice were partitioned into four leave-mice-out folds (Table~\ref{app:tab:splits}, \apdx{app:data:splits}).
Joint scope pools all non-held-out mice from laboratories 2, 3, and 5 into one population model and tests on the held-out fold across sites.
Within-lab scope restricts both training and validation to a single laboratory with the same fold assignments.
Fold~4 is the primary benchmark for macro metrics and the holdout $K$-sweep because it balances held-out load across sites after moving one Copenhagen mouse out of that fold (\apdx{app:data:splits}).
The holdout $K$-sweep trains on fourteen mice and evaluates on six held-out fold~4 mice from Bern, Copenhagen, and Lyon under leave-mice-out cross-validation.
The population $K$-sweep pools all twenty mice in one model with the same locked recipe and retains Artifact epochs in the training stream.
Training and validation both use the full cohort rather than mouse holdout.
Staging at inference uses only the encoder and mixture prior without expert labels.
Macro NMI against Wake, NREM, and REM is computed only after checkpoint selection for post-hoc evaluation.
Per-laboratory spectral locks remain fixed at holdout.
Agreement with expert Wake, NREM, and REM is prior NMI,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:nmi}
\mathrm{NMI}(Y,\hat{Y}) = \frac{2\,I(Y;\hat{Y})}{H(Y)+H(\hat{Y})},
\end{equation}
where $I$ is mutual information and $H$ Shannon entropy.
We use NMI as the primary metric because predicted mixture indices are arbitrary up to permutation, whereas macro-aligned accuracy first maps each cluster to a dominant expert stage.
NMI scores partition agreement without that alignment step and remains defined when mixture count exceeds three, but its interpretation weakens when expert labels stay three-class. Mixture count is selected from prior log-density instead (Substage resolution and physiology).
Each fold trains three seeds.
Holdout prior NMI uses the best unsupervised checkpoint per seed and reports the maximum across seeds, matching incohort ablation reporting but optimising relative to seed means (Limitations).
Macro-aligned accuracy, Cohen's $\kappa$, decoder-only ablation, and substage sweep setup are in Appendix~B and~C.

\papersection{Results and discussion}

\papersubsection{Cross-laboratory holdout at macro resolution}

We evaluated decoder-only cGMVAE and cHMM--GMVAE under joint leave-mice-out training on twenty quality-screened mice from Bern, Copenhagen, and Lyon (Table~\ref{table1}).
cGMVAE, HMMGMVAE, and cHMM--GMVAE were trained with the same locked preprocessing and CNN--VAE hyperparameters per laboratory, differing in prior type, sequence length ($T{=}1$ versus $T{=}64$), and subject-embedding use. cGMVAE versus cHMM--GMVAE tests the temporal prior, and HMMGMVAE versus cHMM--GMVAE tests decoder-only subject conditioning at matched $T{=}64$.
Although supervised pipelines on MSSV reach much higher accuracy when retrained per site or given long temporal context~\cite{miladinovic2019spindle,wang2026rest,garciaciudad2024temporal}, we report unsupervised performance under that matched recipe.
cHMM--GMVAE reached mean prior NMI 0.575 versus 0.282 for cGMVAE, a gain of 0.29 averaged over four folds.

Within-lab training exceeds joint training at every site. On held-out Bern mice, for example, cGMVAE prior NMI averages 0.22 under joint training versus 0.55 within-laboratory on the same animals.
This pattern is consistent with residual covariate shift when pooling heterogeneous spectral profiles across sites.
Decoder-only subject embeddings absorb amplitude nuisance during reconstruction but do not fully remove equipment and scoring context, so site-specific mismatch can still hurt encoder-based staging at holdout.
Macro-aligned accuracy tells a complementary story.
On held-out Copenhagen mice, cHMM--GMVAE reaches about 92\% under joint training and about 94\% within-laboratory (Table~\ref{table2}), and Lyon joint accuracy is about 88\%.
Those values sit near published expert inter-scorer agreement on rodent EEG/EMG macro epochs (about 95--96\% within a laboratory and about 90\% across laboratories on non-artifact Wake, NREM, and REM~\cite{miladinovic2019spindle}).
Against single-rater expert macros used for evaluation, accuracy in the low-to-mid 90\% range indicates strong coarse-stage alignment, with REM and transitions as the main remaining disagreement modes.

\papersubsection{Substage resolution and physiology}

For population substage discovery, expert Wake, NREM, and REM are a post-hoc reference for NMI, not a claim that sleep contains only three dynamical regimes.
Rodent sleep biology motivates finer partitions than three macros: NREM may split into light and deep substages (lower $\delta$ with sparse spindles versus dominant synchronized slow waves, analogous to human N1--N3), Wake into quiet and active substates, REM occasionally into phasic and tonic components, plus short-bout transition buckets and measurement-artefact epochs.
We select mixture count $K$ from mean normalised prior log-density on the population sweep, measuring how well the encoder and temporal mixture prior fit validation latents under the unsupervised checkpoint rule. Macro prior NMI is computed only after that choice and is not used to pick $K$.

\papersubsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Our agreement metric is prior NMI against expert macro labels.
Those labels are single-rater and disagree across MSSV sites, especially for REM and transitions~\cite{rose2026consensus}.
Within the same experimental design, cross-laboratory holdout remains harder than within-laboratory training on the same mice (Table~\ref{table2}).
For substages, the holdout sweep uses fold~4 only with Artifact epochs removed, whereas population $K$ sweeps train on all twenty mice with Artifact epochs retained and support physiology interpretation rather than leave-mice-out generalisation claims.
Headline NMI further uses the best of three seeds per fold, which is optimistic relative to seed means.
Proposed substages at $K{=}8$ are interpreted from unsupervised physiology and transitions and should be read as hypothesis-generating rather than as validated clinical stage names.

\papersection{Conclusion}

Cross-laboratory mouse sleep can be staged without labels or per-mouse calibration, but only coarsely today. Temporal mixture priors improve Wake/NREM alignment on held-out mice relative to a static mixture prior, with mean prior NMI 0.575 versus 0.282 (+0.29 over folds). Expert labels evaluate coarse alignment only. On the full cohort, an eight-substage map at $K{=}8$ combines the strongest population prior fit with separable physiology and transition structure, suggesting structure beyond Wake, NREM, and REM alone. Together, these results support label-free cross-laboratory staging and a proposed substage map beyond three expert macro classes, without requiring harmonised scoring across sites.

\subsection{HMM--GMM prior (equations)}
\label{app:ablations:hmm}

Each discrete latent state $s_t \in \{1,\ldots,K\}$ emits a diagonal Gaussian in $\mathbb{R}^{d}$.
The per-step emission log-density is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:emission}
\log b_k(\mathbf{z}_t) = \log \mathcal{N}\!\left(\mathbf{z}_t \mid \mathbf{m}_k, \operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{v}_k)\right).
\end{equation}
Sticky transitions use self-transition weight $\kappa{=}0.92$.
The marginal log-likelihood of a latent window is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:forward}
\log p(\mathbf{z}_{1:T}) = \log \sum_{s_{1:T}} \pi_{s_1} \prod_{t=2}^{T} A_{s_{t-1}, s_t} \prod_{t=1}^{T} b_{s_t}(\mathbf{z}_t),
\end{equation}
which enters the training KL term $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KL}}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:kl_hmm}.
Warm-start phases and CNN widths are in \apdx{app:methods:warmstart} and \apdx{app:methods:hyper}.
Controlled synthetic datasets in the MSc thesis validated MAR-HMM optimisation before MSSV experiments (thesis Ch.~3). That validation is not repeated here.
