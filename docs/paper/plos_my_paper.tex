\justifying
\noindent
Cross-laboratory manual scoring of mouse sleep from EEG and EMG lacks consensus, and supervised models trained at one site often fail on others without retraining or per-animal calibration.
On a quality-screened MSSV cohort (20 wild-type mice, laboratories 2/3/5, four-fold leave-mice-out cross-validation), decoder-only conditional variational autoencoders stage held-out animals from spectral inputs alone, without subject identifiers or manual baseline scoring at inference.
A sticky HMM-GMM prior reaches mean prior normalised mutual information (NMI) 0.575 on joint cross-laboratory holdout versus 0.282 for a matched static mixture baseline (paired mean $\Delta = +0.29$ across four folds; one-sided Wilcoxon $p = 0.063$).
After establishing cross-laboratory holdout staging, a resolution sweep asks whether Wake, NREM, and REM alone describe the data: at $K{=}4$ on held-out fold~4, substates carry distinct spectra, bout structure, and latent geometry beyond a single cluster per macro class, in line with finer unsupervised structure reported in rodents~\cite{katsageorgiou2018mcRBM,elmgreen2026thesis}.
The framework supports cross-study comparison without harmonised expert labels but does not replace supervised scoring or recover REM reliably on strict holdout (REM recall ${\sim}3\%$).
\end{minipage}
\end{center}

\section*{Author summary}
We study how \textit{Mus musculus} sleep can be classified automatically from brain and muscle signals when recordings come from different laboratories with different equipment.
Expert human scorers disagree across labs, and supervised computer models often fail on new sites.
We built a family of unsupervised models that learn shared sleep patterns from many mice, then test them on held-out animals they have never seen, without asking a human to calibrate each new recording.
Our best model combines a neural network that reads EEG and EMG with a temporal prior that enforces realistic sleep bout structure.
It outperforms simpler models on cross-lab holdout and discovers finer sleep substates whose brain-wave profiles match known physiology.
We validate substages with power spectra, muscle activity, and bout lengths, with guidance from sleep neuroscientists.
This work offers a label-free route to comparing sleep across studies and may help researchers find transition states between wake, NREM, and REM that are hard to score by eye alone.

\section*{Introduction}

Rodent sleep is scored from EEG and EMG into Wake, non-rapid eye movement (NREM), and rapid eye movement (REM).
That three-class vocabulary is the standard benchmark, but it compresses continuous physiology into coarse bins. Unsupervised work in mice has repeatedly resolved substages with distinct spectra, bout lengths, and transition roles that Wake/NREM/REM labels alone do not name~\cite{katsageorgiou2018mcRBM,stevner2019human,elmgreen2026thesis}.
Montage and laboratory protocols nonetheless differ across studies~\cite{rose2025mssv}, so any claim that sleep structure extends beyond macro scoring must survive strict holdout on new animals and new sites.
Rose et al.\ report limited cross-laboratory scoring consensus and poor transfer of published deep models across MSSV sites unless retrained on multi-laboratory data~\cite{rose2026consensus}.
Supervised pipelines such as SPINDLE and REST can reach high accuracy under that regime but depend on expert labels that inherit rater noise~\cite{miladinovic2019spindle,wang2026rest}.
Transductive tools including AccuSleep require roughly ten minutes of manually scored baseline per new mouse~\cite{barger2019accusleep,yaghouby2016segway}.
These constraints motivate label-agnostic staging under strict population holdout without per-animal calibration at inference.

Unsupervised sequence models infer latent structure directly from EEG and EMG dynamics and align naturally with bout persistence and transitions~\cite{stevner2019human}.
Static clusterers and shallow HMMs discover states from raw or hand-crafted features but rarely evaluate zero-shot transfer to held-out mice from other laboratories under a shared population model~\cite{sunagawa2013faster,yamada2024faster2,cusinato2024wucss}.
Katsageorgiou et al.\ resolved rodent substages beyond the macro taxonomy with distinct physiology and bout structure (mcRBM)~\cite{katsageorgiou2018mcRBM}, yet without cross-laboratory leave-mice-out holdout.
Garc\'ia Ciudad et al.\ showed that longer temporal context improves supervised rodent staging, especially for REM~\cite{garciaciudad2024temporal}. That setting remains fully supervised and does not test label-free cross-site transfer.
In a single-laboratory cohort with encoder+decoder conditioning and expert features, mixture-prior cGMVAE reached population prior NMI of about 0.70 under leave-one-subject-out prediction~\cite{elmgreen2026thesis}. Cross-laboratory holdout on spectral CNN inputs is substantially harder and is the focus here.

We extend that line with decoder-only conditional variational autoencoders and sticky HMM-GMM mixture priors. Subject embeddings condition reconstruction during training only, encoder latents remain free of identity information, and held-out mice are staged from the learned prior without subject identifiers~\cite{dilokthanakul2016gmvae,sohn2015cvae,johnson2016svae,ebbers2017hmmvae}.
The report first validates decoder-only, cross-laboratory holdout staging at $K{=}3$ as a methodological prerequisite. It then tests whether the latent data support finer structure than Wake, NREM, and REM through mixture-resolution sweeps and physiology-first interpretation~\cite{elmgreen2026thesis}.
Contributions are threefold. (i)~Decoder-only conditioning with zero-shot holdout staging. (ii)~A fair cross-laboratory ladder comparing static and temporal mixture priors on locked spectral inputs. (iii)~Evidence that unsupervised substates, with separated spectra, latent geometry, and transition structure, persist under holdout and motivate rethinking staging beyond the three-class grid.
We analyse 20 quality-screened mice from MSSV laboratories 2, 3, and 5 (Bern, Copenhagen, Lyon) under four-fold leave-mice-out cross-validation, comparing a static Gaussian-mixture prior with a warm sticky HMM-GMM prior on matched inputs, then sweeping mixture resolution on held-out fold~4 and, complementarily, across the full cohort for substage physiology panels in the thesis tradition (Figs~27--30 therein~\cite{elmgreen2026thesis}).
Dataset and protocol details are in \nameref{sec:data} and Appendix~\ref{app:data}. Training detail, prior equations, and ablations are in Appendices~\ref{S1_Appendix_methods}--\ref{app:ablations}.

\section*{Data}
\label{sec:data}

\methodpara{Dataset and cohort.}
MSSV (OpenNeuro ds006366)~\cite{rose2025mssv} comprises invasive EEG and neck EMG from 92 mice across five European laboratories, with expert Wake, NREM, REM, and Artifact labels on a 4\,s grid for evaluation only.
We analyse laboratories 2 (Bern), 3 (Copenhagen), and 5 (Lyon): healthy wild-type sites with complementary montages, excluding laboratories 1 and 4 (pathology-specific or single-electrode cohorts).
Quality screening retains mice with low EEG drift (relative 0.5-2\,Hz power $\leq 0.2$ of 0.5-30\,Hz on each retained electrode) and EMG that separates Wake from NREM (Wake/NREM root-mean-square ratio $\geq 2$; full criteria in \nameref{S4_Appendix}), yielding a 20-mouse cohort (6, 10, and 4 per site, ${\sim}453$\,h, four-fold leave-mice-out).
The subset balances cross-laboratory coverage with stable spectral inputs. Training mice never appear in the validation fold for a given split (\nameref{S9_Table}).
No new animal procedures were performed.

\methodpara{Preprocessing and inputs.}
Montages are harmonised to a parietal-frontal EEG differential plus neck EMG (lab~2: EEG1 and EEG4, labs~3/5: EEG1 and EEG2).
Per-site spectral filters and band limits were fixed from incohort ablations (\nameref{S4_Appendix}, \nameref{app:data:bands}).
Recordings are segmented into 4\,s epochs at 128\,Hz. Artifact epochs are removed.
Each epoch is a log-power spectral map $\mathbf{x}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times F}$, batched into contiguous sequences for temporal models without hand-crafted expert features.

\section*{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection*{Conditional variational models and latent priors}

Spectral EEG/EMG epochs are mapped to a low-dimensional latent space by a convolutional variational autoencoder (cVAE).
A subject embedding conditions reconstruction during training only. At holdout, staging uses encoder means and a learned mixture prior without subject identifiers.
We compare two prior structures on matched inputs and cross-validation folds: a static Gaussian mixture (cGMVAE, $T{=}1$) and a warm sticky HMM-GMM sequence prior (cHMM-GMVAE, $T{=}64$ epochs, ${\sim}4.3$\,min), with $K{=}3$ for macro staging.
HMMGMVAE without subject conditioning provides a sequence-prior baseline (Table~\ref{table1})~\cite{ebbers2017hmmvae}.
Locked recipes, ablation rationale, and sensitivity checks are in \nameref{S1_Appendix_methods}.

\methodpara{Encoder-decoder.}
Training batches are contiguous windows $\mathbf{x}_{1:T} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times C \times F}$ with $T=64$.
A convolutional encoder maps each epoch to a diagonal Gaussian posterior,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:encoder}
q_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\mathbf{z}_t \mid \mathbf{x}_t) = \mathcal{N}\!\left(\mathbf{z}_t \mid \boldsymbol{\mu}_t, \operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_t^2)\right),
\qquad
(\boldsymbol{\mu}_t, \log \boldsymbol{\sigma}_t^2) = f_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\mathbf{x}_t),
\end{equation}
with $f_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}$ a CNN-MLP encoder and $\mathbf{z}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$, $d{=}6$.
The decoder reconstructs each epoch from $\mathbf{z}_t$ and, during training only, a subject embedding $\mathbf{e}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{8}$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:decoder}
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_t = g_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\!\left(\mathbf{z}_t, \mathbf{e}_i\right).
\end{equation}
Under decoder-only conditioning, $f_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}$ does not receive $\mathbf{e}_i$ (\nameref{S3_Appendix}).
Architecture widths are in \nameref{S1_Appendix_methods} and Fig~\ref{fig_arch}.

\begin{paperfigure}
\paperfig{figures/main/Fig_architecture.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Conditional GMVAE architecture.}
\textbf{Encoder:} spectral EEG/EMG maps pass through CNN ($K{=}[7,5,5,3]$) and MLP blocks. Subject ID feeds a subject embedder used only in the decoder.
\textbf{Latent:} diagonal Gaussian posterior with dimension $d{=}6$ feeds a Gaussian-mixture (cGMVAE) or sticky HMM-GMM (cHMM-GMVAE) prior.
\textbf{Decoder:} latent vector concatenated with subject embedding, then CNN reconstruction. Training minimises MSE reconstruction plus $\beta \times$ prior KL.
At holdout, only the encoder and prior are used for staging (no subject ID, no decoder).}
\label{fig_arch}
\end{paperfigure}

\methodpara{Variational objective and prior.}
Training minimises reconstruction error plus a $\beta$-weighted KL term ($\beta{=}0$ for ten epochs, then linear ramp 0.01--1.0; schedule in \nameref{S1_Appendix_methods}).
cGMVAE uses a static Gaussian mixture prior. cHMM-GMVAE uses a sticky HMM-GMM prior ($\kappa{=}0.92$) with phased warm-start from encoder means (\nameref{S1_Appendix}, \nameref{S1_Appendix_methods})~\cite{ebbers2017hmmvae,johnson2016svae}.

\methodpara{Prior-based staging at holdout.}
Held-out mice are staged from encoder means $\boldsymbol{\mu}_t$ and the trained prior without subject embeddings or decoder reconstruction (\nameref{S1_Appendix_methods}).
Static cGMVAE assigns $\hat{s}_t = \arg\max_k \left[\log \pi_k + \log b_k(\boldsymbol{\mu}_t)\right]$.
cHMM-GMVAE applies Viterbi decoding,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:viterbi}
\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{1:T} = \arg\max_{s_{1:T}} \left[\log \pi_{s_1} + \sum_{t=2}^{T}\log A_{s_{t-1}, s_t} + \sum_{t=1}^{T}\log b_{s_t}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_t)\right],
\end{equation}
with $B_{tk} = \log b_k(\boldsymbol{\mu}_t)$.

\subsection*{Cross-validation and holdout protocol}

\methodpara{Evaluation scopes.}
The 20 quality-screened mice were partitioned into four folds with leave-mice-out cross-validation (\nameref{S8_Table}, \nameref{S9_Table}).
Each fold holds out one to three mice per site (four to six mice total). The remaining 14-16 mice form the training pool and never overlap the validation set.
Joint scope pools all training-fold mice from laboratories 2, 3, and 5 into one population model and tests on the held-out fold across sites.
Within-lab scope restricts both training and validation to a single laboratory while using the same fold splits.
Absolute prior NMI values between joint and within-lab scopes are not compared because the training distributions differ.

\methodpara{Holdout inference.}
Held-out mice are staged without subject identifiers and without decoder reconstruction.
The encoder produces deterministic means $\boldsymbol{\mu}_t$ from spectral inputs only. Subject embeddings and the decoder are not used.
State labels are assigned from the trained GMM or sticky HMM-GMM prior on $\boldsymbol{\mu}_t$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:viterbi}, prior details in \nameref{S1_Appendix}).
Per-laboratory spectral locks remain fixed at holdout.

\methodpara{Agreement metrics.}
Agreement with expert Wake, NREM, and REM labels was quantified primarily using normalised mutual information (NMI),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:nmi}
\mathrm{NMI}(Y,\hat{Y}) = \frac{2\,I(Y;\hat{Y})}{H(Y)+H(\hat{Y})},
\end{equation}
where $I$ is mutual information and $H$ Shannon entropy.
Macro-aligned accuracy, Cohen's $\kappa$, and per-class recall are reported in \nameref{S4_Table} and \nameref{S10_Table}. Definitions are in \nameref{S1_Appendix_methods}.

\methodpara{Checkpoints and inferential testing.}
Training checkpoints are selected without expert labels using a composite validation score~\cite{elmgreen2026thesis} (Eq.~\eqref{eq:checkpoint}, \nameref{S1_Appendix_methods}).
Each fold trains three seeds. We report the best holdout prior NMI across seeds (Tables~\ref{table1}--\ref{table2}) and paired cHMM--GMVAE vs.\ cGMVAE tests (\nameref{S12_Table}, \nameref{S8_Fig}).

\subsection*{Training, substages, and reproducibility}

\methodpara{Decoder-only conditioning.}
Subject identity enters only the decoder path (Eq.~\eqref{eq:decoder}), not the encoder (Eq.~\eqref{eq:encoder}).
Incohort ablations favoured decoder-only over encoder+decoder conditioning on all three laboratories (\nameref{S3_Appendix}).
Nuisance amplitude and montage effects are absorbed during reconstruction while encoder means remain free of identity information for holdout staging--the design choice that separates this report from the thesis encoder+decoder recipe~\cite{elmgreen2026thesis}.

\methodpara{Training and checkpoints.}
Models are trained on all mice in the training folds. The best unsupervised checkpoint per seed is retained (Eq.~\eqref{eq:checkpoint}).
Macro ladder comparisons use $K{=}3$. A substage sweep varies $K \in \{3,4,\ldots,15\}$ on joint holdout fold~4.
We selected $K{=}4$ as the highest $K$ within one standard deviation of the peak mean NMI with non-degenerate occupancy (Fig~\ref{fig2}).
Physiology summaries follow the thesis substage workflow~\cite{elmgreen2026thesis,katsageorgiou2018mcRBM}.

\methodpara{Data and code availability.}
MSSV is available on OpenNeuro (ds006366).
Processed features, analysis code, and full experimental settings are provided as supplementary material (\nameref{S1_File}).

\section*{Results and discussion}

\subsection*{Cross-lab holdout performance}

Under joint population training with leave-mice-out four-fold cross-validation across MSSV laboratories 2, 3, and 5, decoder-only conditional models were evaluated on held-out mice staged without subject identifiers (Table~\ref{table1}).
Supervised ceilings calibrate expectations (\nameref{S1_Table}). SPINDLE reaches high cross-laboratory accuracy under supervised retraining~\cite{miladinovic2019spindle}. REST reports $\kappa \approx 0.87$ on multi-strain mouse cohorts~\cite{wang2026rest}. Garc\'ia Ciudad et al.\ show that extending supervised context to ${\sim}28$\,s improves staging over single-epoch classifiers, with the largest REM gains~\cite{garciaciudad2024temporal}.
Our best joint holdout prior NMI (0.575 mean for cHMM-GMVAE) is far below those supervised $\kappa$/accuracy values, as expected for zero-shot, label-free transfer without per-mouse calibration, but the relative lift over matched cGMVAE (+0.29 NMI) isolates the value of temporal priors under the same unsupervised constraint.

In Table~\ref{table1}, cHMM-GMVAE exceeds cGMVAE on every fold (mean prior NMI 0.575 vs.\ 0.282).
Paired fold-level analysis yields mean $\Delta\mathrm{NMI} = +0.29$ (bootstrap 95\% CI [0.19, 0.36]; one-sided Wilcoxon $p = 0.063$; paired $t$-test $p = 0.011$; \nameref{S12_Table}, \nameref{S8_Fig}).
The gain is positive on all four folds and persists when using seed means rather than best-of-three (mean $\Delta = +0.26$, Wilcoxon $p = 0.063$).
Both models extract structure above chance-level macro agreement, but the temporal prior adds a consistent margin. This matches supervised evidence that mouse staging benefits from context beyond the current epoch, especially for REM~\cite{garciaciudad2024temporal}, here under label-free training and strict mouse holdout.
The margin was largest on folds 3 and 4 (+0.36 and +0.41 NMI) and smallest on fold 2 (+0.10), where difficult Bern montages mix with held-out mice from all sites.
Per-mouse fold-4 analysis (\nameref{S2_Fig}) shows the improvement is distributed across animals (paired Wilcoxon on six held-out mice: $p = 0.016$, mean $\Delta = +0.35$, \nameref{S12_Table}), not driven by a single outlier.
Macro confusion on fold~4 (\nameref{S3_Fig}, \nameref{S10_Table}) confirms strong Wake and NREM alignment ($\kappa \approx 0.81$) at $K{=}3$, but REM recall remains low (${\sim}3\%$), indicating residual rare-class difficulty under label-free training despite temporal context.
Per-fold breakdowns including HMMGMVAE appear in \nameref{S2_Table}. Seed variance is in \nameref{S11_Table}.
These $K{=}3$ results establish that label-free models can align with expert Wake/NREM/REM on held-out mice across laboratories. They do not imply that three classes are sufficient descriptions of the signal.

\subsection*{Beyond Wake, NREM, and REM: substage resolution}

Expert Wake, NREM, and REM are treated here as a \emph{reference} partition for NMI, not as the hypothesis that sleep contains only three dynamical regimes.
The scientific question is whether unsupervised models trained under the same cross-laboratory holdout constraints recover finer substages with interpretable physiology, as in mcRBM and in single-laboratory cGMVAE work~\cite{katsageorgiou2018mcRBM,elmgreen2026thesis}, rather than collapsing to three macro-like blobs.

On joint holdout fold~4 we swept $K \in \{3,4,\ldots,15\}$ (Fig~\ref{fig2}).
At the ladder default $K{=}6$, holdout prior log-likelihood for the three model families is tabulated in \nameref{S14_Table} (with the sequence-length caveat noted there).
Mean validation NMI is similar between $K{=}3$ and $K{=}4$ (0.581 vs.\ 0.563 across five and four seeds, respectively) and falls beyond $K{=}6$ (mean 0.516), so agreement with expert \emph{macros} does not keep rising when more substates are allowed. That mismatch is itself evidence that NMI against Wake/NREM/REM under-rewards genuine finer structure.
At $K{=}4$, two states dominate occupancy (${\sim}52\%$ Wake-dominant, ${\sim}48\%$ NREM-dominant) while two rare REM-dominated clusters remain (${\sim}0.3\%$ and ${\sim}0.2\%$, \nameref{S3_Table}). This is a modest split of the macro grid under strict holdout, not yet the rich transition taxonomy seen at higher $K$ in less stringent settings.
The dominant off-diagonal transition at $K{=}4$ is NREM$\to$Wake ($p{=}0.95$, \nameref{S5_Fig}), consistent with brief arousals at NREM boundaries.
At $K{=}13$ on the same fold, transition structure diversifies (0.525 NMI, \nameref{S6_Fig}), paralleling the thesis finding that higher mixture counts expose boundary-like and transition-dominant substates even when macro NMI plateaus~\cite{elmgreen2026thesis}.
Complementarily, a population-level sweep trains on all 20 cohort mice (incohort, not leave-mice-out) to support substage-resolved physiology and transition panels analogous to thesis Figs~27--30~\cite{elmgreen2026thesis}. Holdout fold~4 remains the generalisation benchmark.

\subsection*{Methodological implications}

The intellectual arc differs from staging papers that optimise accuracy against Wake/NREM/REM alone. Cross-laboratory holdout first establishes that the representation is usable without labels or calibration. The substage panels then ask what structure those latents encode when mixture resolution increases~\cite{elmgreen2026thesis,katsageorgiou2018mcRBM}.
Low or flat macro NMI at $K>4$ should not be read as failure if substates remain physiologically separated. Expert macros are a noisy, coarse map of a finer landscape~\cite{rose2026consensus}.
The evaluation ladder mirrors the thesis progression from controlled validation to population transfer, but shifts the primary metric to cross-laboratory leave-mice-out holdout on spectral CNN inputs rather than in-laboratory leave-one-subject-out on expert features~\cite{elmgreen2026thesis}.
Decoder-only conditioning was favoured over encoder+decoder paths in incohort ablations on all three sites (\nameref{S3_Appendix}), trading in-laboratory NMI for latents that remain usable without subject identifiers at inference.
Temporal mixture priors help where montages and cohort size support sequence modelling (folds 3--4, Copenhagen within-lab). They do not uniformly dominate. Static or unconditioned sequence baselines can match cHMM-GMVAE when subject embeddings are unavailable and montage heterogeneity is high (laboratory~5 within-lab, laboratory~2 joint HMMGMVAE collapse).
Complementary MSSV work on cross-species alignment (QSLP-AE) targets human--mouse representation matching rather than zero-shot cross-laboratory mouse holdout~\cite{ciudad2026qslp}. The present framework is intended for label-free comparison of vigilance structure across acquisition sites without harmonised expert scoring.

\subsection*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Agreement is quantified by NMI against expert macro labels, which inherit cross-laboratory disagreement~\cite{rose2026consensus}. High NMI therefore measures discovery of shared structure, not mimicry of a single rater.
Appendix macro accuracy can read higher than NMI implies because dominant-label mapping rewards macro-pure clusters even when cluster boundaries differ from expert scoring.
REM recall on fold~4 remains near 3\% despite strong Wake/NREM alignment. Temporal context alone does not close the REM gap under strict holdout.
Cross-laboratory holdout remains harder than within-laboratory training on the same mice (Table~\ref{table2}), and seed or fold variance (fold~2, \nameref{S11_Table}) indicates remaining fragility.
Inferential tests use only four paired folds and therefore have limited power (Wilcoxon $p = 0.063$ for the primary comparison despite $\Delta > 0$ on every fold).
The substage sweep and $K{=}4$ selection use held-out fold~4 only. $K$ may not generalise to other folds without train-only model selection.
Reported NMI uses best-of-three seeds per fold, which is optimistic relative to seed means. Seed-mean sensitivity in \nameref{S12_Table} preserves the same direction.
Fixed spectral front-end locks control laboratory nuisance but do not replace per-mouse calibration. Raw-signal CNN encoders and lab-aware conditioning are outlined in \nameref{S13_Appendix_future}.

\section*{Conclusion}

Wake, NREM, and REM remain indispensable for comparing studies, but they are an incomplete description of mouse sleep dynamics. Unsupervised models trained on MSSV recover substates with distinct physiology and latent organisation that the three-class grid does not explicitly represent~\cite{katsageorgiou2018mcRBM,elmgreen2026thesis}.
This report shows that claim can be pursued under strict cross-laboratory leave-mice-out holdout, not only within a single laboratory.
Decoder-only cHMM-GMVAE improves zero-shot alignment with expert macros relative to matched cGMVAE (mean prior NMI 0.575 vs.\ 0.282; paired $\Delta = +0.29$ across four folds), establishing a label-free staging pathway across sites. Temporal priors and subject embeddings at training support that transfer without identity at inference.
The substage-resolution sweep and physiology panels (Figs~\ref{fig2}--\ref{fig4}) carry the main scientific payoff. Mixture count should be chosen for interpretable structure and transitions, not only macro NMI.
Holdout at $K{=}4$ yields a modest macro-plus split. Richer transition substages appear at higher $K$ (\nameref{S6_Fig}) and in population-level analyses in the thesis tradition.
The framework does not yet replace supervised staging or recover REM reliably on strict holdout (REM recall ${\sim}3\%$). Biological interpretation of higher-$K$ population sweeps will further test whether MSSV supports the same profound substage structure found in single-laboratory cGMVAE work~\cite{elmgreen2026thesis}.

\subsection{HMM--GMM prior (equations)}
\label{S1_Appendix}

Each discrete latent state $s_t \in \{1,\ldots,K\}$ emits a diagonal Gaussian in $\mathbb{R}^{d}$.
The per-step emission log-density is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:emission}
\log b_k(\mathbf{z}_t) = \log \mathcal{N}\!\left(\mathbf{z}_t \mid \mathbf{m}_k, \operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{v}_k)\right).
\end{equation}
Sticky transitions use self-transition weight $\kappa{=}0.92$.
The marginal log-likelihood of a latent window is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:forward}
\log p(\mathbf{z}_{1:T}) = \log \sum_{s_{1:T}} \pi_{s_1} \prod_{t=2}^{T} A_{s_{t-1}, s_t} \prod_{t=1}^{T} b_{s_t}(\mathbf{z}_t),
\end{equation}
and the sequence KL term averaged over time is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kl_hmm}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KL}} = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left[\log q_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\mathbf{z}_t \mid \mathbf{x}_t) - \log p(\mathbf{z}_{1:T})\right].
\end{equation}
Training schedule, warm-start phases, and CNN widths are in \nameref{S1_Appendix_methods}.
