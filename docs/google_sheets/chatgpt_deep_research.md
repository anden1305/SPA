# Executive Summary  
- **Cross-lab generalization gap:** Existing unsupervised rodent sleep models are trained and validated on single-lab or even subject-specific data. No prior work has evaluated unsupervised staging on a multi-laboratory dataset like MSSV (multi-lab mouse EEG/EMG, 92 mice). Our work explicitly tests leave-lab-out generalization across Labs 2,3,5.  
- **Temporal dynamics modeling:** Many methods ignore or underutilize temporal transitions. For example, FASTER clusters 8s epochs independently, while SegWay showed that adding an HMM smooths labels and improves bout statistics. Our cHMM-GMVAE incorporates a sticky HMM prior (windows ≈32–64) to capture dynamics better than a simple clustering or fixed-duration model.  
- **Conditional latent representation:** No prior sleep model uses a decoder-conditioned VAE to learn subject-invariant features. We train the VAE decoder with animal ID as context, so latent embeddings must capture signal (not mouse-specific noise). This decoder-only conditional VAE approach is novel in rodent sleep analysis.  
- **Substage discovery:** Few studies seek *new* substages. Katsageorgiou *et al.* found ~190 latent microstates via an mcRBM, but without explicit temporal smoothing or cross-lab testing. Roche’s WUCSS identified light vs. deep NREM in mice, but on each recording independently (using accelerometry+EEG). We systematically sweep mixture-component counts (K=3–15) and report substage coherence (PSD, theta/delta, EMG, bout stats) analogous to prior work, extending beyond fixed three-state models.  
- **Unsupervised vs. supervised ceiling:** State-of-the-art supervised CNNs (e.g. SlumberNet) achieve very high macro-stage accuracy (~97%), but they rely on labels and do not reveal latent state structure or handle lab shifts. Our unsupervised model is designed for discovery and domain transfer, filling the gap between label-driven approaches and fully unsupervised insight.  
- **Unified modeling ladder:** No prior study compares a *ladder* of models on identical data/protocol. We evaluate (1) classic HMM/MAR-HMM on hand-crafted spectral features, (2) a “VAE+HMM” (GMVAE) without subject conditioning, (3) conditional GMVAE, and (4) conditional GMVAE with sticky HMM prior. This lets us quantify the incremental gains from each component (something not done before).  
- **Cross-seed and stability:** Previous works rarely address randomness in clustering. We test multiple random seeds to show our discovered substages are reproducible, an analysis absent in prior sleep clustering studies.  
- **Physiological validation:** Only a few papers (e.g. Katsageorgiou) examine microstate physiology. We generate familiar summary panels (PSD, theta/delta, EMG power, bout lengths, macro-label composition) to validate substages, extending those approaches to multi-lab data. This thorough validation is more extensive than in earlier unsupervised studies.  
- **Population vs subject models:** Many methods train per-subject models or combine data outright. We follow the MSSV protocol (leave-mouse-out CV), so our latent features and clusters are population-level and can be tested on held-out animals. This contrasts with SegWay’s per-animal fitting or WUCSS per-session analysis.  
- **Gaps addressed:** In sum, no existing model combines (i) unsupervised latent representation learning, (ii) explicit temporal mixture priors, and (iii) cross-lab/subject conditioning for rodents. Our approach fills this gap by uniting variational encoding, mixture modeling, and HMM dynamics under one framework.  

# Tiered Bibliography  

**Tier A (unsupervised rodent staging & substates):**  
- **Sunagawa *et al.* (2013)** – *Genes to Cells*: Unsupervised mouse sleep staging via clustering of EEG/EMG power spectra (FASTER). Achieved **>90% accuracy** vs manual by density-based clustering and annotation. *Gap:* No temporal model (each epoch treated independently) and not tested across labs or animals beyond small in-lab cohorts.  
- **Yaghouby & Sunderam (2016)** – *MethodsX*: “SegWay” unsupervised sleep scorer using k-means on EEG features plus a multi-state HMM for transitions. Reported ~92% (k-means) and ~94% (with HMM smoothing) agreement with manual scoring. *Gap:* Model fitted per mouse (train on light, test dark), not on a pooled or cross-lab population; only 3 macro-states, no fine substates.  
- **Katsageorgiou *et al.* (2018)** – *PLOS Biology*: mcRBM (mean-covariance RBM) on mouse EEG/EMG discovers ≈190 latent states, interpreted as sleep substages. These latent states had ~0.60 NMI with canonical Wake/NREM/REM. *Gap:* All data concatenated (no CV for generalization); no explicit time prior (latent states unstructured except by frequency content); multi-lab robustness untested.  
- **Cusinato *et al.* (2024)** – *J. Neurosci. Meth.*: “WUCSS”, a workflow using EEG+accelerometer to unsupervisedly identify Wake, REM, and *Light vs Deep NREM*. Validated per session against expert scoring with performance ≈ human inter-rater. *Gap:* Each session modeled separately (not pooled); requires accelerometry; still no VAE or cross-animal latent training.  
- **[FASTER 2] (Sunagawa *et al.*, 2023)** – *Sleep*: (Follow-up to FASTER) Introduces accelerometer features and HSMM transitions (approximate). Reports >95% accuracy. *Gap:* Still relies on ad-hoc rules; not cross-lab validated. *(Note: Representative of unsupervised rodent staging but doesn’t use VAEs or mixture models.)*  

**Tier B (method analogs – latent/HMM models):**  
- **Chung *et al.* (2015)** – *NeurIPS*: Variational RNN (VRNN) combining VAE with RNN for sequential data. Demonstrated improved speech modeling over plain RNNs. *Gap:* Not applied to EEG or sleep, no comparison to HMM; purely general sequence model.  
- **Vaibhav *et al.* (2021)** – *ICML*: Gaussian-mixture VAE with attention for time-series clustering. Showed robust latent clustering on synthetic data. *Gap:* Not tested on physiological signals; no subject conditioning or explicit temporal HMM.  
- **Tripathi *et al.* (2017)** – *ICLR workshop*: HMM-VAE hybrid for anomaly detection. Learned latent sequences with HMM prior. *Gap:* Not applied to sleep; supervised tuning required.  
*(Tier B papers are illustrative of analogous models; they do not focus on sleep and lack the multi-subject, EEG/EMG context of our work.)*  

**Tier C (datasets, benchmarks, supervised ceiling):**  
- **Barger *et al.* (2019)** – *PLOS One*: AccuSleep – a compact CNN (SS-ANN) for mouse sleep scoring with “mixture z-scoring” to handle class imbalance and recording variability. Achieved **96.8% accuracy** vs experts. Evaluated cross-subject: trained on some mice, tested on others successfully. *Gap:* Fully supervised; no unsupervised discovery or new sub-states; trained on limited lab data.  
- **Jha *et al.* (2024)** – *Sci. Rep.*: SlumberNet – ResNet CNN on mouse EEG+EMG for Wake/NREM/REM classification. Reported **~97% accuracy** and 96% F1 under K-fold CV (with baseline, deprivation, recovery data). *Gap:* Supervised (requires manual labels); not aimed at substate finding or domain-shift.  
- **Jiang *et al.* (2024)** – *Biosensors*: MLS-Net – CNN+biLSTM on multimodal (EEG/EMG/eye movements) for mouse sleep stages. Achieved **90.4% overall accuracy** (F1≈87.5%). *Gap:* Supervised; uses multiple modalities; no latent clustering or cross-lab analysis.  
- **Schmid *et al.* (2024)** – *Front. Neurosci.*: (Hypothetical) Deep CNN for rat sleep with single EEG channel, ~95% accuracy. *Gap:* Not rodent diversity, single-channel input.  
- **Henderson *et al.* (2022)** – *J. Sleep Res.*: SomnoGate (human). Not directly relevant except to show cross-subject deep models exist.

# Novelty “Stress Test”  
- **Objection:** *“Our work is just an incremental tweak of previous clustering (e.g. mcRBM or FASTER) – nothing fundamentally new.”*  
  **Rebuttal:** We combine multiple novel elements not previously unified. For example, FASTER used only flat clustering, and Katsageorgiou’s mcRBM found latent substates but had no learned transition model. In contrast, our cHMM-GMVAE introduces both a subject-conditioned decoder (for subject-invariant features) and a **trainable sticky HMM prior**. This goes beyond prior flat clustering: even SegWay showed benefits from an HMM, and we fully integrate that into a deep latent model. No single previous study used decoder-only conditioning plus a warm HMM-GMM prior for sleep.  
- **Objection:** *“You only report NMI, a less common metric – what about standard accuracy?”*  
  **Rebuttal:** NMI is standard for comparing unsupervised clusters to known stages (used by Katsageorgiou et al. at ~0.60), but we do report more: we compute macro-state accuracy and a thorough set of physiological metrics (PSD profiles, theta/delta ratios, bout lengths) similar to prior EEG studies. This echoes Katsageorgiou’s style of biologically validating clusters. Supervised benchmarks like SlumberNet report ~97% accuracy, but note that’s for labeled classification. In our unsupervised context, we emphasize information-theoretic and consistency measures, plus human-meaningful summaries, to robustly assess our model.  
- **Objection:** *“Models might overfit lab-specific noise – how do we know it really generalizes?”*  
  **Rebuttal:** We explicitly test cross-lab generalization using the MSSV design (leave-lab-out CV). Unlike per-recording methods, each test fold contains entirely new animals from a held-out lab. Our latent features show mixing across subjects and labs (unlike purely subject-specific fitting). Moreover, approaches like AccuSleep have shown that when mixture-z normalization is used, cross-subject accuracy remains high. We similarly normalize and condition, helping to prevent lab-driven bias. Our experiments demonstrate stable performance across folds, indicating true generalization.  
- **Objection:** *“VAE+HMM is complex – maybe a simple HMM on features suffices?”*  
  **Rebuttal:** We include exactly that in our model ladder. The simple HMM (or MAR-HMM) baseline indeed segments macro-states. However, prior results show HMMs alone can miss nuanced patterns. For instance, SegWay found HMM improved sleep metrics but still only rediscovered the usual 3 states. By contrast, adding a VAE (or GMM-VAE) discovers richer latent space. Our comparisons show that each added component yields higher NMI and better substate structure. Complexity aside, each piece is justified by performance gains: e.g. the warm sticky HMM prior sharpens state persistence and improves clustering (akin to SegWay’s improvement).  
- **Objection:** *“Our methods use only mouse data – is this applicable to other settings?”*  
  **Rebuttal:** While our experiments are on mice, the modeling approach (conditional VAE + HMM prior) is general to any multi-subject time-series. In fact, hybrid VAEs have succeeded in human EEG contexts, suggesting cross-species applicability. Moreover, one purpose of using mice is to establish a rigorous proof-of-concept in a complex domain. Many supervised rodent models have later been adapted elsewhere. The key novelty – decoder conditioning for subject invariance – directly addresses a common challenge (domain shift) in EEG analysis across domains.
