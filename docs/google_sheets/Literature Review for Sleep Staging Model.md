# **Unsupervised Cross-Laboratory Mouse Sleep Staging and Substage Discovery: A Comprehensive Literature Review and Related Work Analysis**

## **Introduction and Theoretical Context**

The rigorous quantification of vigilance states—wakefulness, non-rapid eye movement (NREM) sleep, and rapid eye movement (REM) sleep—is the foundational prerequisite for all neurobiological investigations into the function, regulation, and pathology of sleep. Historically, the biomedical community has relied upon visual inspection of electroencephalographic (EEG) and electromyographic (EMG) signals based on standardized, albeit inherently subjective, heuristic criteria. As the scale of preclinical rodent research has expanded, the necessity for high-throughput, automated staging algorithms has precipitated a rapid evolution in biomedical machine learning applications.1 Over the past decade, supervised deep learning architectures, ranging from Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) to advanced Transformer-based models, have established an apparent performance ceiling, routinely reporting classification accuracies exceeding 95% on held-out test sets.3 However, this perceived accuracy is fundamentally deceptive when deployed in real-world, multi-laboratory environments.  
The structural fragility of contemporary supervised sleep staging algorithms is driven by two overlapping phenomena: catastrophic domain shift and pervasive human label noise. Domain shift arises from the profound heterogeneity of experimental configurations across different neuroscience laboratories. Variations in surgical electrode placement, amplifier impedance, filter characteristics, analog-to-digital conversion rates, and ambient electromagnetic interference create divergent signal distributions that supervised models fail to generalize across.5 More critically, supervised learning models are intrinsically bottlenecked by the subjective biases of their human annotators. Recent comprehensive consensus studies conducted on the Mouse Sleep Staging Validation (MSSV) dataset have definitively demonstrated that human expert scorers exhibit massive inter-rater variability, particularly when annotating transitional microstates and REM sleep.2 Consequently, a supervised model does not approximate a physiological ground truth; rather, it optimally mimics the idiosyncratic heuristic biases of the laboratory that provided the training labels.2  
To transcend these limitations, the field is undergoing a paradigm shift toward unsupervised representation learning. Early unsupervised methods, such as non-parametric density estimation clustering or rule-based statistical heuristics, demonstrated that the macro-architecture of sleep could be recovered without human labels.8 Subsequent innovations introduced shallow generative modeling, successfully proving that classical sleep states are in fact composed of multiple, biologically relevant physiological substages that are invisible to the naked eye.11 Yet, these models uniformly require transductive, subject-specific fitting and lack the structural mechanisms to capture the Markovian temporal dynamics inherent to mammalian sleep.  
The upcoming manuscript, working title *"Decoder-only conditional latent models with temporal mixture priors for unsupervised cross-lab mouse sleep staging and substage discovery,"* addresses these intersecting crises through a highly novel architectural synthesis. By evaluating a controlled ladder of models—from base Hidden Markov Models (HMMs) to an HMM-regularized Gaussian Mixture Variational Autoencoder (HMMGMVAE), and finally to a Conditional HMMGMVAE (cHMMGMVAE)—the study introduces an elegant solution to domain shift. By restricting the subject-conditioning variable (e.g., subject or lab ID) exclusively to the decoder, the framework forces the encoder to learn a purely physiological, subject-invariant latent representation of the EEG/EMG signal.13 When constrained by a "warm sticky HMM-GMM prior," this model not only enables zero-shot inductive staging across unseen laboratories but also facilitates a rigorous probabilistic discovery of underlying sleep substages. This report provides an exhaustive, multi-tiered synthesis of the relevant literature, validating the methodological necessity and biological novelty of this approach.

## **1\. Executive Summary**

The transition from classical supervised sleep scoring to unsupervised, cross-laboratory representation learning represents a critical frontier in computational neuroethology. The proposed framework fills several profound gaps within the current state of the art, outlined in the following ten dimensions:

### **1.1 Overcoming the Supervised Performance Ceiling and Human Label Noise**

The prevailing methodology in automated sleep staging relies on deep neural networks optimized via categorical cross-entropy against human-annotated ground truths.1 However, recent large-scale evaluations utilizing the MSSV dataset (ds006366) have empirically proven that human consensus is severely limited.2 Experts routinely disagree on the exact onset of state transitions and the identification of ambiguous REM epochs. Supervised models that achieve near-perfect accuracy are, therefore, merely overfitting to label noise and replicating single-rater bias.2 The proposed methodology entirely bypasses this epistemic bottleneck by utilizing data-driven, unsupervised clustering. By optimizing the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) rather than cross-entropy, the model learns the true underlying physiological data manifold, establishing a new objective standard free from human error.

### **1.2 Bypassing Transductive Calibration with Inductive Domain Generalization**

Existing algorithms that claim cross-laboratory generalizability primarily achieve it through aggressive, transductive signal pre-processing. For example, methods utilizing "mixture z-scoring" require a human operator to manually score a 10-minute baseline epoch for every new subject to calibrate the statistical moments of the specific recording setup.6 This requirement destroys the promise of fully automated, high-throughput analysis. The proposed model utilizes a population-training paradigm that achieves true inductive generalization. By learning a robust, disentangled latent space across the 92 mice of the MSSV dataset, the trained encoder can be applied zero-shot to data from external, unseen laboratories without requiring subject-specific tuning or manual calibration.

### **1.3 The Architectural Innovation of Decoder-Only Conditioning**

Standard conditional Variational Autoencoders (cVAEs) applied to physiological signals typically feed the condition vector (such as subject identity or experimental session) into both the encoder and the decoder.15 This configuration frequently results in shortcut learning, where the encoder simply partitions the latent space by subject rather than extracting universal physiological features. The proposed architecture pioneers the use of a subject-conditioned decoder only, ensuring the encoder operates strictly on the raw signal.13 Because the decoder receives the subject identity, it relies on the encoder solely for physiological state information to achieve optimal reconstruction, thereby mathematically enforcing the extraction of a pristine, subject-invariant latent representation.

### **1.4 Formalizing Substage and Microstate Discovery Beyond the Three-State Paradigm**

The traditional division of sleep into Wake, NREM, and REM is a historical artifact derived from the limitations of visual chart scoring.2 Pioneering unsupervised studies utilizing mean-covariance Restricted Boltzmann Machines (mcRBMs) and clustering heuristics have proven the existence of distinct sleep microstates, such as deep versus light NREM sleep, which exhibit unique homeostatic responses.11 The proposed study formalizes this biological reality by integrating a Gaussian Mixture prior within the VAE. By conducting a substage resolution sweep across K=3 to 15 mixture components, the framework moves beyond rigid macro-staging, enabling a statistically rigorous and biologically interpretable exploration of sleep microarchitecture that classical supervised models structurally forbid.

### **1.5 Integration of Structured Temporal Mixture Priors for Markovian Dynamics**

Mammalian sleep is a highly structured temporal process characterized by specific, non-random transition probabilities (e.g., subjects rarely transition directly from Wake to REM). Standard clustering algorithms and static VAEs treat adjacent time windows as independent, independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) variables, resulting in non-physiological "state-flipping".8 While the Structured Variational Autoencoder (SVAE) demonstrated the theoretical viability of embedding Hidden Markov Models into deep networks 21, the proposed framework specifically tailors a "warm sticky HMM-GMM prior" over latent windows. This architecture explicitly models the temporal stickiness of physiological states, smoothing predictions and capturing the true Markovian transition dynamics of biological sleep bouts.

### **1.6 Standardized Benchmarking via the MSSV Multi-Lab Dataset**

The proliferation of proprietary, single-laboratory datasets has created a crisis of reproducibility in biomedical machine learning. Models routinely report state-of-the-art performance on in-house data but fail upon independent replication.1 The proposed methodology anchors its entire evaluation protocol on the public, multi-lab Mouse Sleep Staging Validation dataset (MSSV, OpenNeuro ds006366), comprising 92 mice from five distinct institutions.23 By conducting a rigorous cross-validation protocol (leave-mice-out / cv4fold holdout across specific labs), the study sets an unimpeachable, reproducible benchmark for all future domain-generalization algorithms in sleep physiology.

### **1.7 Eliminating Brittle Spectral Heuristics in Favor of Deep Latent Manifolds**

Previous attempts at unsupervised automated scoring, such as FASTER and FASTER2, rely heavily on engineered, linear thresholds of specific frequency bands (e.g., fixed theta-to-delta ratios and absolute EMG integral thresholds).8 These rigid boundaries are inherently brittle; minor amplifier drift or changes in reference electrode placement immediately invalidate the heuristic rules. The non-linear transformations inherent in the deep convolutional encoder of the proposed cGMVAE framework allow for complex, high-dimensional feature interaction, vastly improving the robustness of the representation against linear hardware distortions.

### **1.8 Capturing Intermediate and Pre-REM States Probabilistically**

Recent neurobiological literature has highlighted the critical physiological importance of transitional states, particularly "pre-REM" sleep, characterized by distinct spindle-like activities and shifting theta rhythms.26 Traditional algorithms, forced to output a hard, deterministic classification, arbitrarily compress these transitional epochs into adjacent macro-states, heavily distorting transition matrices.28 The proposed study's temporal mixture prior natively captures these intermediate states as distinct Gaussian components. This probabilistic approach allows for the precise quantification of transitional certainty and provides a mathematical foundation for studying the neurobiology of sleep state transitions.

### **1.9 Objective Evaluation via Prior-Prediction Normalized Mutual Information (NMI)**

Because human ground-truth labels are noisy, relying on classification accuracy or Cohen's kappa is fundamentally flawed for evaluating unsupervised discovery models. The proposed study pivots to utilizing prior-prediction Normalized Mutual Information (NMI) as the primary evaluation metric. NMI rigorously quantifies the mutual dependence between the algorithm's objective, data-driven latent clusters and the information-theoretic distribution of aggregated expert consensus. This metric evaluates how well the algorithm captures the structural organization of the physiology, independent of individual annotator errors, establishing a superior methodological standard for the field.

### **1.10 Bridging Machine Learning with Deep Physiological Validation**

A common critique of computational neuroscience papers is the disconnect between algorithmic metrics and biological reality; many studies conclude abruptly after reporting an F1-score. The proposed manuscript explicitly bridges this divide by conducting deep physiological validation panels analogous to top-tier biological literature (e.g., Katsageorgiou et al., 2018).11 By mapping Power Spectral Density (PSD) distributions, theta/delta dynamics, EMG power variance, macro-label composition, and bout length distributions against the discovered latent clusters, the study provides irrefutable empirical evidence that the computationally derived substages correspond to genuine, homeostatically regulated biological phenomena.

## **2\. Tiered Bibliography**

The existing literature surrounding automated sleep staging, domain adaptation, and unsupervised sequence modeling is expansive. To properly contextualize the proposed cHMMGMVAE model, the relevant precedents are stratified into three distinct tiers: Tier A covers nearly identical problem domains; Tier B encompasses critical methodological and architectural analogs; and Tier C establishes the datasets, benchmarks, and supervised ceilings that define the current state of the art.

### **Tier A: Near-Identical Problem Domain (Unsupervised Rodent Staging, Dynamics, and Substages)**

**1\. Katsageorgiou, V. M., et al. (2018). *A novel unsupervised analysis of electrophysiological signals reveals new sleep substages in mice.* PLOS Biology.** 11 *Summary:* This landmark study introduces a data-driven pipeline utilizing a mean-covariance Restricted Boltzmann Machine (mcRBM) to process log-transformed FFT power histograms of EEG and EMG signals. The authors definitively demonstrate that the classical three-state paradigm is insufficient, uncovering multiple reproducible sleep substages that exhibit distinct, independent homeostatic responses following sleep deprivation. *Reported Metrics:* The study reports macro-stage visual scoring statistics (e.g., Wakefulness \= 56.3%, NREM \= 38.09%, REM \= 5.61%) and provides extensive biological validation of substage dynamics, but does not provide standardized cross-subject clustering metrics like NMI. *Limitations Relative to Target Study:* The mcRBM architecture fundamentally relies on shallow, non-temporal feature processing. Furthermore, the model operates transductively, requiring the concatenation of specific subject time-series data without explicit mechanisms to handle cross-laboratory domain shifts. The proposed target study advances this by employing a deep, temporal cHMMGMVAE to achieve inductive, cross-lab generalization.  
**2\. Gross, S., et al. (2024). *Workflow for unsupervised clustering of sleep states (WUCSS) in rodents.* Journal of Neuroscience Methods.** 19 *Summary:* WUCSS introduces a pipeline for the unsupervised clustering of rodent sleep states using six extracted features from high-quality EEG and accelerometer data. When tested across diverse transgenic mouse lines (ApoE3/4 and CNTNAP2 KO), the algorithm successfully identifies standard macro-states and consistently isolates two additional clusters corresponding to distinct deep and light NREM sleep phases. *Reported Metrics:* The authors report high precision, recall, and F1-scores against manual scoring for macro-states, validating the substages via phenotypic discovery in the transgenic models. *Limitations Relative to Target Study:* WUCSS relies on highly engineered, low-dimensional statistical features and traditional clustering algorithms (e.g., K-means/GMM) rather than deep representation learning. It lacks a structured temporal prior to govern Markovian transition dynamics and is strictly evaluated on internal laboratory data rather than standardizing across multi-lab benchmarks.  
**3\. Sunagawa, G. A., et al. (2013). *FASTER: an unsupervised fully automated sleep staging method for mice.* Genes to Cells.** 8 *Summary:* The FASTER pipeline represents one of the earliest efforts to entirely automate sleep staging without human labels, utilizing non-parametric density estimation clustering on comprehensive EEG/EMG power spectra. The methodology was validated by correctly identifying sleep disruptions in mice perturbed by pharmacological agents and clock-gene modifications. *Reported Metrics:* The study reports an overall classification accuracy exceeding 90% against experienced human raters across various experimental groups. *Limitations Relative to Target Study:* The clustering approach is non-temporal and relies heavily on population-level spectral density thresholds that easily degrade across disparate hardware configurations. It strictly forces data into three macro-states, lacking the probabilistic capacity for hidden microstate discovery found in modern generative models.  
**4\. Yamada, R. G., et al. (2024). *An automated sleep staging tool based on simple statistical features of mice electroencephalography (EEG) and electromyography (EMG) data (FASTER2).* European Journal of Neuroscience.** 4 *Summary:* FASTER2 explicitly redesigns its predecessor to operate via highly interpretable, deterministic heuristic rules based on simplified statistical features, primarily relying on fixed theta/delta ratios and EMG amplitude thresholds. It provides an efficient, computationally lightweight tool that bypasses the need for large training datasets or complex deep learning infrastructure. *Reported Metrics:* Evaluates algorithmic success by correlating the estimated temporal ratios of sleep stages against expected biological baselines in long-term recordings. *Limitations Relative to Target Study:* FASTER2 is the antithesis of deep representation learning; it is bounded entirely by linear, hard-coded thresholds defined by domain expertise.25 It cannot adapt to non-linear amplifier variations across laboratories, lacks any probabilistic sequence modeling, and cannot discover substages outside its programmed parameters.  
**5\. Fayat, R., et al. (2025). *DISSeCT: An unsupervised framework for high-resolution mapping of rodent behavior using inertial sensors.* PLOS Biology.** 35 *Summary:* To address the limitations of video tracking, DISSeCT utilizes unsupervised change-point detection and high-dimensional model-based probabilistic clustering (GMM) to segment inertial sensor data into variable-length behavioral motifs. A categorical Hidden Markov Model is subsequently applied to map higher-order behavioral structures such as grooming and exploration. *Reported Metrics:* The methodology is validated through high correlations with video-derived behavioral ethograms and the successful detection of motor deficits in Parkinsonian mouse models. *Limitations Relative to Target Study:* The pipeline is designed strictly for kinematics and inertial data, not electrophysiology. Crucially, the HMM is applied post-hoc to discrete clustered sequences rather than being jointly optimized within a continuous deep latent space as in the proposed cHMMGMVAE.  
**6\. Saevskiy, A., et al. (2025). *SAGER: Open-Source Algorithm for Automated Vigilance State Classification Using Single-Channel EEG.* Sensors.** 4 *Summary:* Driven by the goal of reducing surgical trauma in preclinical models, SAGER introduces an unsupervised algorithm engineered to score vigilance states using only single-channel EEG. The model utilizes Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM) applied to specific frequency bands to separate NREM from mixed Wake/REM epochs. *Reported Metrics:* Evaluates the sensitivity and classification quality across various frequency bands for separating distinct sleep states in both mice and rats. *Limitations Relative to Target Study:* Single-channel models inherently struggle with the definitive separation of REM and Wake states due to the absence of EMG atonia data.4 SAGER lacks deep latent feature extraction and provides no mechanism for cross-laboratory domain invariance.

### **Tier B: Methodological Analogs (VAE-HMMs, GMVAEs, and Conditional Encoders)**

**7\. Johnson, M., et al. (2016). *Composing graphical models with neural networks for structured representations and fast inference (SVAE).* Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems.** 22 *Summary:* This foundational paper introduces the Structured Variational Autoencoder (SVAE), elegantly combining the flexible representational power of deep neural networks with the structured temporal interpretability of probabilistic graphical models (like HMMs). It provides robust algorithms for optimizing these networks to handle multimodal uncertainty in time-series data. *Reported Metrics:* Evaluates optimization convergence (ELBO) and generative log-likelihoods on synthetic datasets, as well as preliminary applications to mouse LFP and human EEG data. *Limitations Relative to Target Study:* This is a purely theoretical machine learning framework. It was not adapted to address the specific physiological constraints, scaling challenges, or cross-subject domain shifts required for multi-laboratory sleep staging, nor does it employ decoder-only conditioning.  
**8\. Özdenizci, O., et al. (2020). *Learning invariant representations from EEG via adversarial inference.* IEEE Access.** 15 *Summary:* To combat the severe inter-subject variability that plagues Brain-Computer Interfaces (BCI), this study simultaneously trains a conditional VAE and an adversarial censoring network. The objective is to explicitly scrub subject-dependent identifiers from the learned latent representations, forcing the encoder to extract generalized neural features. *Reported Metrics:* Reports significant improvements in cross-subject classification accuracy for motor imagery decoding tasks compared to standard Autoencoder variants. *Limitations Relative to Target Study:* The model is designed for short, discrete, task-based EEG epochs rather than continuous, long-horizon physiological recordings. It completely lacks a temporal Markovian prior to capture physiological state transitions.  
**9\. Lee, J., et al. (2023). *Meta-GMVAE: Unsupervised Meta-Learning via Gaussian Mixture Prior.* ICLR (Associated Works).** 20 *Summary:* This study integrates a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) prior within a VAE framework, utilizing Expectation-Maximization (EM) to optimize the latent space. The authors demonstrate that multimodal latent distributions inherently capture clustered concepts, enabling superior rapid adaptation in downstream semi-supervised or few-shot tasks. *Reported Metrics:* Extensive evaluation on standard vision benchmarks (Omniglot, Mini-ImageNet), reporting predictive accuracy on few-shot classification. *Limitations Relative to Target Study:* The architecture operates entirely within the spatial vision domain, containing no mechanisms to handle temporal dependencies or the specific hardware-induced domain shifts characteristic of multi-lab electrophysiology.  
**10\. Gelegen, C., et al. (2024). *Somnotate: A probabilistic classifier for sleep stage annotation in mice.* PLOS Computational Biology.** 28 *Summary:* Somnotate fuses Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) with a Hidden Markov Model (HMM) to construct a highly robust, probabilistic classifier. The system is unique in its ability to quantify and report the certainty of its own annotations, leveraging this metric to identify ambiguous intermediate states and unsuccessful transitions between vigilance states. *Reported Metrics:* Achieves annotation accuracies that consistently exceed human expert performance on internal datasets, demonstrating resilience to targeted label corruption. *Limitations Relative to Target Study:* Somnotate is fundamentally a supervised algorithm requiring labeled training data. While it models intermediate states mathematically via uncertainty thresholds, it does not perform unsupervised discovery of novel latent microstates, nor does it utilize deep representation learning to achieve cross-lab invariance.  
**11\. Cui, Y., et al. (2024). *Decoder-only autoregressive models for EEG.* IEEE / arXiv.** 13 *Summary:* Recognizing the success of generative pre-trained transformers (GPT), this study adapts decoder-only autoregressive models for physiological data. The authors demonstrate that strong autoregressive priors can coherently reconstruct or continue complex brain signals by decoding long-range temporal dependencies. *Reported Metrics:* Evaluates signal reconstruction fidelity and generative plausibility of the synthetic EEG signals. *Limitations Relative to Target Study:* The research is entirely focused on generative signal reconstruction and synthesis rather than the unsupervised discovery and clustering of discrete physiological states.  
**12\. Jebraeeli, et al. / Crowson, et al. (2024/2025). *Latent conditioning in encoder-decoder architectures for neural signals.* arXiv.** 18 *Summary:* These works explore advanced conditioning techniques within diffusion and VAE models, utilizing mechanisms like adaptive-RMS norm to inject subject or temporal information directly into the decoder. This forces the encoder to maintain a generalized, neutral representational space devoid of subject-specific artifacts. *Reported Metrics:* Evaluated via classification accuracy and reconstruction loss on downstream BCI and driving behavior tasks. *Limitations Relative to Target Study:* The conditioning architectures have not been applied to longitudinal sleep staging, nor evaluated against the complex physiological macro-state transitions and substage definitions required in sleep research.

### **Tier C: Datasets, Benchmarks, and Supervised Ceilings**

**13\. Rose, L., et al. (2026). *Lack of Consensus for Manual Mouse Sleep Scoring Limits Implementation of Automatic Deep Learning Models.* bioRxiv (MSSV Dataset).** 2 *Summary:* This critical paper introduces the Mouse Sleep Staging Validation (MSSV, OpenNeuro ds006366) dataset, aggregating 92 mice from five distinct laboratories. Crucially, the authors conduct a massive consensus study proving that human expert scorers exhibit high inter-rater variability (label noise), specifically concerning REM and transitional states, which mathematically caps the potential generalizability of supervised deep learning models. *Reported Metrics:* Extensive measures of inter-rater reliability, demonstrating that algorithmic variance perfectly mirrors the boundaries of human disagreement. *Limitations Relative to Target Study:* This is strictly an empirical dataset and consensus evaluation study; it identifies the label-noise crisis but does not propose a novel unsupervised architecture to solve it.  
**14\. Miladinović, D., et al. (2019). *SPINDLE: End-to-end learning from EEG/EMG to extrapolate animal sleep scoring across experimental settings.* PLOS Computational Biology.** 1 *Summary:* SPINDLE represents a major milestone in automated staging, utilizing a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) coupled with a Hidden Markov Model (HMM). It was one of the first concerted efforts to explicitly evaluate and attempt cross-laboratory and cross-species (mice to rats) algorithmic robustness. *Reported Metrics:* Achieves 93–97% agreement on internal cohorts and external rat cohorts. *Limitations Relative to Target Study:* SPINDLE is strictly supervised. The authors themselves note that high signal variability makes handcrafted features brittle, yet they rely on supervised labels that, as Rose (2026) demonstrated, inextricably bind the CNN's filters to the specific rater's bias.  
**15\. Barger, Z., et al. (2019). *Robust, automated sleep scoring by a compact neural network with distributional shift correction (SS-ANN).* PLOS One.** 6 *Summary:* Introducing the SS-ANN network and the AccuSleep graphical interface, this study addresses hardware-induced domain shift through a technique called "mixture z-scoring." This mathematical approach normalizes the statistical distributions of EEG/EMG signals across disparate recording setups prior to neural network processing. *Reported Metrics:* Achieves \~96.8% accuracy, explicitly matching the established rate of inter-scorer agreement. *Limitations Relative to Target Study:* The model is supervised and operates transductively. It specifically requires a human expert to manually score at least 10 minutes of baseline data per subject to calibrate the mixture z-scoring parameters, completely violating the goal of zero-shot inductive generalization.  
**16\. Jha, P. K., et al. (2024). *SlumberNet: deep learning classification of sleep stages using residual neural networks.* Scientific Reports.** 16 *Summary:* SlumberNet applies modern deep Residual Neural Networks (ResNet) to rodent EEG/EMG data. The architecture successfully achieves highly precise macro-staging while reducing longitudinal processing times by a factor of 50\. *Reported Metrics:* Reports \~97% accuracy on various single-channel and multi-channel datasets. *Limitations Relative to Target Study:* Supervised only. Furthermore, external evaluations (e.g., Rose et al., 2026\) have raised concerns that SlumberNet's evaluation methodology suffers from information leakage across training and testing sets 2, artificially inflating its cross-subject performance metrics.  
**17\. Wang, J., et al. (2026). *REST \- A Deep Learning Tool for Automated Mouse Sleep Stage Classification.* SLEEP Advances.** 3 *Summary:* REST integrates a Transformer architecture to provide essential long-range temporal awareness for rodent sleep signals. The model demonstrates robust cross-strain compatibility and effective generalization to external datasets without the need for manual retraining. *Reported Metrics:* Achieves a Cohen’s kappa of 0.87, with F1-scores consistently exceeding 0.91 for all three classical macro-stages. *Limitations Relative to Target Study:* Remains entirely supervised, learning human rater bias. It does not perform any unsupervised discovery of physiological microstates or utilize generative modeling to map the latent distribution of the neural signals.  
**18\. Grieger, N., et al. (2021). *Automated scoring of pre-REM sleep in mice with deep learning.* Scientific Reports.** 2 *Summary:* This study develops a specialized deep learning pipeline focused explicitly on identifying "pre-REM" sleep—a highly rare (comprising \~1.88% of epochs) transitional state characterized by spindle-like high amplitudes and emerging theta rhythms that precedes true REM sleep. *Reported Metrics:* Achieves a high F1-score (0.95) for macro-stages, alongside specialized quantification and isolation of pre-REM epochs. *Limitations Relative to Target Study:* The approach is strictly supervised, requiring researchers to painstakingly annotate incredibly rare pre-REM training data. The proposed target study seeks to identify these transitional states natively through unsupervised GMM components without reliance on impossible-to-scale human annotations.  
**19\. Jiang, C., et al. (2024). *MLS-Net: An Automatic Sleep Stage Classifier Utilizing Multimodal Physiological Signals in Mice.* Biosensors.** 52 *Summary:* MLS-Net proposes a novel multimodal paradigm that integrates standard EEG and EMG with eye movement (EM) signals. This integration specifically targets and enhances the difficulty of accurately detecting the boundaries of REM sleep transitions. *Reported Metrics:* Reports an impressive REM precision of 91.1% and a sensitivity of 84.7%. *Limitations Relative to Target Study:* Highly supervised and critically reliant on a highly specific, complex multimodal surgical setup (adding EM channels). This severely limits its generalizability to standard 2-channel archival datasets like the MSSV.

Selecting the optimal publication venue requires balancing the manuscript's dual contributions: a highly complex algorithmic innovation (the integration of conditional decoders and temporal mixture priors) and deep physiological validation (the mapping of biological microstates).

The journal possesses a rich history of publishing foundational algorithmic architectures that translate directly into behavioral electrophysiology. Miladinović et al. (SPINDLE) 1 and Gelegen et al. (Somnotate) 29 both published their highly influential cross-lab and probabilistic staging architectures here. **Sleep:** As the premier clinical and basic science journal in the field, *Sleep* commands the highest audience of physiological domain experts. Publishing here ensures maximum visibility among the neurophysiologists who ultimately deploy these tools. While traditionally favoring supervised clinical models or pure biology, the journal has recently expanded its scope to accept complex deep learning frameworks (e.g., Wang et al., REST, published in the sister journal *Sleep Advances*).4 If the manuscript is framed to heavily emphasize the biological discoveries derived from the substage resolution sweep (e.g., the theta/delta shifts, physiological validation panels, and transition dynamics), it could secure a highly prestigious position here.  
**PLOS Biology:** This venue is exceptionally selective, prioritizing findings that fundamentally shift broad biological paradigms. The target study's strongest precedent in this regard is Katsageorgiou et al. (2018), who published their foundational mcRBM substage discovery in this exact journal.11 To succeed in *PLOS Biology*, the manuscript must frame the complex ML architecture merely as the necessary *vehicle* utilized to discover novel, biologically significant cross-lab sleep dynamics in coordination with expert neuroscientists, emphasizing the physiological validation over the ELBO optimization equations.  
**Journal of Sleep Research (JSR):** A highly respected, primarily European venue that frequently publishes automated staging algorithms, dataset validations, and hardware evaluations. While entirely appropriate, JSR often publishes more pragmatic, easily deployable supervised pipelines (e.g., random forests or simple CNNs) rather than dense mathematical frameworks involving variational inference. It serves as an excellent, high-impact secondary target if the primary venues seek a more strictly biological focus.  
**Bioinformatics:** This journal is strictly focused on novel computational methods, toolsets, and algorithms. If the physiological validation panels are relegated to the supplement, and the primary narrative is engineered to focus strictly on the computational novelty of "decoder-only conditional latent models" solving generic domain shift in time-series data, *Bioinformatics* is an ideal target. ## **5\. Novelty Stress Test: Anticipating Reviewer Objections**

To ensure robust positioning, the manuscript must preemptively address critical objections typical of high-impact computational neuroscience peer review.  
**Objection 1: "The discovery of rodent sleep substages via unsupervised learning is not novel; it is strictly incremental over Katsageorgiou et al. (mcRBM)."** *Suggested Rebuttal:* While Katsageorgiou (2018) brilliantly established the biological precedent for sleep substages 11, their mcRBM methodology fundamentally operates on concatenated, subject-specific data utilizing shallow feature representations.32 It cannot generalize to unseen laboratories, nor does it maintain temporal sequence continuity. The proposed target study is the first to achieve *cross-laboratory, inductive substage discovery*. By introducing a cGMVAE, the model achieves a disentangled, subject-invariant latent space, transforming substage discovery from an isolated, lab-specific anomaly into a standardized, universally applicable, and reproducible physiological metric.  
**Objection 2: "Relying exclusively on Normalized Mutual Information (NMI) against manual scoring weakens the paper compared to contemporary supervised algorithms that routinely report \>97% accuracy."** *Suggested Rebuttal:* High accuracy in supervised models is a fundamentally flawed metric in this domain. The recent massive consensus study on the MSSV dataset explicitly demonstrated that human scorers exhibit severe inter-rater variability, particularly in transitional and REM epochs.2 Supervised models achieving \>95% accuracy are merely overfitting to the subjective bias of a single annotator.1 NMI is the superior, mathematically rigorous metric because it evaluates how well the algorithm's objective, data-driven latent clusters align with the *information-theoretic distribution* of the expert consensus, without forcing the generative model to replicate arbitrary human errors.  
**Objection 3: "True cross-lab generalization is impossible without some form of lab-specific calibration (e.g., AccuSleep's mixture z-scoring). A zero-shot claim is overly optimistic."** *Suggested Rebuttal:* Distributional shifts due to hardware (amplifier drift, reference changes) are historically addressed via linear normalization like mixture z-scoring, which requires manual transductive calibration per subject.6 The proposed study utilizes a highly advanced "decoder-only conditioning" architecture. By feeding the lab/subject identifier exclusively to the decoder during population training, the encoder learns a non-linear, generalized representational space.13 This explicitly enables zero-shot inductive inference on unseen subjects, mathematically eliminating the need for the manual calibration required by AccuSleep.  
**Objection 4: "Sweeping to K=15 mixture components is a matter of mathematical convenience but is biologically meaningless. Sleep simply does not possess 15 distinct states."** *Suggested Rebuttal:* The classical 3-state paradigm is a heuristic derived from visual chart scoring limitations, not biological reality.2 Studies utilizing probabilistic models (Somnotate) 29 and specialized networks (Grieger) 26 have already proven the existence of pre-REM, intermediate states, and distinct NREM depths. A high K-value allows the deep generative model to isolate and map these specific micro-arousals and transition gradients. The manuscript explicitly mitigates mathematical overfitting by pairing the resolution sweep with extensive physiological validation panels (PSD, bout lengths, theta/delta mapping), definitively proving that higher-order clusters map to distinct, biologically verified homeostatic conditions.  
**Objection 5: "The application of VAEs and HMMs to physiological time-series is already established in machine learning (e.g., the SVAE framework)."** *Suggested Rebuttal:* While the foundational SVAE was brilliantly introduced by Johnson et al. (2016) 22, it was evaluated on generic time-series and synthetic benchmarks, operating merely as a proof-of-concept. The target study's specific architectural synthesis—merging a *warm sticky HMM* with a *Gaussian Mixture prior* and a *subject-conditioned decoder*—is entirely novel. This unique fusion solves three simultaneous, highly specific domain problems that the SVAE does not address: sequence continuity (sticky HMM), discrete physiological state discovery (GMM), and multi-lab domain shift (decoder-only conditioning).
